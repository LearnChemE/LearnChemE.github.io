Two-Site Ion Exchange Isotherm

Directions

This simulation plots the equilibrium isotherm for ion exchange of Cs+ in solution with Na+ on crystalline silicotitanate (CST), an inorganic ion exchanger used to remove radioactive cesium from nuclear waste. The solid has two types of exchange sites, and each site has its own selectivity coefficient KC. The solid black curve is the overall isotherm; it is the sum of the contributions of the two sites (dashed curves).

Use the KC1 and KC2 sliders to set the selectivity coefficient of each site (log scale from 0.01 to 1000). The third slider sets how the exchange capacity is split between the sites: the left end is only site 1, the right end is only site 2, and the fraction of capacity in site 2 is shown beside the slider.

The grey dashed line is the isotherm for KC = 1 (no preference for either ion). Click or drag on the plot to move the red point; hold the mouse over the point to show the liquid and solid equivalent fractions there. The initial slope of the isotherm (the Henry's law constant) is shown on the plot. Uncheck "show site contributions" to show only the overall isotherm.

Details

Cesium in solution exchanges with sodium in the solid (both ions have a +1 charge):

\[ \mathrm{Cs^+} + \overline{\mathrm{Na^+}} \rightleftharpoons \overline{\mathrm{Cs^+}} + \mathrm{Na^+} \]

where the overbar denotes the solid phase. The selectivity coefficient of a site, written with equivalent fractions, is

\[ K_C = \frac{Q_{Cs}\,C_{Na}}{Q_{Na}\,C_{Cs}} = \frac{\bar{X}_{Cs}\,(1-X_{Cs})}{(1-\bar{X}_{Cs})\,X_{Cs}} \]

The equivalent fractions in the liquid and solid are

\[ X_{Cs} = \frac{C_{Cs}}{C_0}, \qquad \bar{X}_{Cs} = \frac{Q_{Cs}}{Q_0} \]

For a single type of site with an ideal solid phase, solving for the solid fraction gives the isotherm

\[ \bar{X}_{Cs} = \frac{K_C\,X_{Cs}}{1+(K_C-1)\,X_{Cs}} \]

which is concave for KC > 1, a straight line for KC = 1, and convex for KC < 1. When two types of sites exchange independently, the isotherm is the capacity-weighted sum of the two single-site isotherms (eq 21 in Zheng et al.):

\[ \bar{X}_{Cs} = \bar{X}_{01}\,\frac{K_{C1}\,X_{Cs}}{1+(K_{C1}-1)\,X_{Cs}} + \bar{X}_{02}\,\frac{K_{C2}\,X_{Cs}}{1+(K_{C2}-1)\,X_{Cs}} \] \[ \bar{X}_{01} + \bar{X}_{02} = 1 \]

Each dashed curve in the plot is one term of this sum. At low cesium concentration the isotherm reduces to Henry's law, with an initial slope equal to the capacity-weighted average selectivity:

\[ \bar{X}_{Cs} \approx \left(\bar{X}_{01}K_{C1} + \bar{X}_{02}K_{C2}\right) X_{Cs} \]

When one site has KC > 1 and the other has KC < 1, the isotherm can have an inflection: the selective site fills first, and the other site loads only at high liquid-phase cesium fractions. For CST, the measured KC for Cs+/Na+ is about 104, so cesium is strongly removed even from waste that is about 5 M in sodium, where XCs is around 10−4.

Reference: Z. Zheng, C. V. Philip, R. G. Anthony, J. L. Krumhansl, D. E. Trudell, and J. E. Miller, "Ion exchange of group I metals by hydrous crystalline silicotitanates," Ind. Eng. Chem. Res. 35 (1996) 4246–4256.

Symbols:

  • \(C_{Cs}, C_{Na}\) = liquid-phase concentrations (equiv/L)
  • \(C_0\) = total liquid-phase concentration, \(C_{Cs}+C_{Na}\) (equiv/L)
  • \(Q_{Cs}, Q_{Na}\) = solid-phase concentrations (equiv/kg)
  • \(Q_0\) = total exchange capacity of the solid (equiv/kg)
  • \(X_{Cs}\) = equivalent fraction of Cs+ in the liquid
  • \(\bar{X}_{Cs}\) = equivalent fraction of Cs+ in the solid
  • \(\bar{X}_{01}, \bar{X}_{02}\) = fraction of exchange capacity in site 1 and site 2
  • \(K_C, K_{C1}, K_{C2}\) = selectivity coefficient (overall, site 1, site 2)
