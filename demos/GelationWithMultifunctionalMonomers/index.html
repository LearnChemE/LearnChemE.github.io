Gelation with Multifunctional Monomers

Directions

This simulation shows how a polymer network (a gel) forms during step-growth polymerization when some of the monomers have more than two reactive groups. Two-functional monomers A–A and B–B make linear chains, but a branch monomer with f A groups (f ≥ 3) links chains together. At a critical conversion, the Flory–Stockmayer gel point, the weight-average molecular weight becomes infinite and an insoluble network appears. Beyond the gel point, the polymer is divided into a soluble fraction (sol) and the network (gel).

Set the functionality f, the branching ratio ρ (fraction of all A groups that are on the f-functional monomer), and the stoichiometric ratio r (A groups/B groups). Then move the conversion slider, or press start to increase conversion automatically and reset to return conversion to zero.

The left plot shows the weight-average (Mw) and number-average (Mn) molecular weights on a log scale. The dots mark the values at the current conversion. Mw diverges at the Flory–Stockmayer gel point (vertical dashed line), while Mn stays finite. Beyond the gel point (shaded) an infinite network (the gel) coexists with the sol (the finite molecules), and the curves are averages only for the sol.

Molecules grow by joining together. Each branch unit adds extra unreacted ends, so the larger a molecule gets, the more ways it has to keep growing. At the gel point, the largest clusters link into one network that spans the whole sample. That network is the gel, a single molecule of effectively infinite molecular weight. Everything not connected to it is the sol. Beyond the gel point, the gel acts as a sink.

The right plot shows the weight fractions of sol and gel. When the gel point conversion would be greater than 1, the system cannot gel.

Why the sol molecular weight decreases after the gel point: the gel has a large number of unreacted groups, so most new bonds connect a sol molecule to the gel. A sol molecule joins the gel when any one of its unreacted ends reacts with it, so the largest, most branched sol molecules are captured first. The sol that remains is mostly small, nearly linear molecules and unreacted monomer, so its Mw and Mn decrease. Mass moves only from sol to gel, so the gel fraction wg keeps increasing; when r = 1, all the material is in the gel at complete conversion.
