Electrical Conductivity of Silicon Semiconductors

material type:
300
2.5

Directions

This Demonstration shows the charge carrier concentration in silicon (Si) and doped Si (n- and p-types) as a function of temperature and dopant concentration. The corresponding electron band configurations are shown to the right of the plot. Phosphorous doping creates n-type Si, whereas boron doping creates p-type Si.

Details

The intrinsic carrier concentration ($C_i$) is:

$$C_i = N e^{-E_g/(2 k_B T)},$$

$$N = N_0 (T/300)^{2.54},$$

where $N$ is the effective density of states (cm$^{-3}$), $E_g$ is the energy gap (eV), $k_B$ is Boltzmann's constant (eV/K), $T$ is temperature (K), and $N_0$ is the effective density of states at 300 K.

The carrier concentration for pure silicon ($C_{Si}$) is:

$$C_{Si} = 5.29\times10^{19}\,(T/300)^{2.54}\,e^{-6729/T}.$$

The carrier concentration for doped silicon ($C_{Si,dop}$) is:

$$C_{Si,dop} = C_{Si} + C_{dop}\, e^{-E/R/T},$$

where $C_{dop}$ is the atomic concentration of dopant atoms, $E$ is the activation energy for the electron of a dopant atom, and $R$ is the ideal gas constant.

All carrier concentrations are in units of cm$^{-3}$.

Symbols

$C_i$ = intrinsic carrier concentration (cm$^{-3}$)
$C_{Si}$ = carrier concentration in pure silicon (cm$^{-3}$)
$C_{Si,dop}$ = carrier concentration in doped silicon (cm$^{-3}$)
$C_{dop}$ = atomic concentration of dopant atoms (cm$^{-3}$)
$N$ = effective density of states (cm$^{-3}$)
$N_0$ = effective density of states at 300 K (cm$^{-3}$)
$E_g$ = energy gap (eV)
$E$ = activation energy for the electron of a dopant atom (J/mol)
$k_B$ = Boltzmann's constant (eV/K)
$R$ = ideal gas constant (J/(mol K))
$T$ = temperature (K)
