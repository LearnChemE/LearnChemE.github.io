Mn = number-average molecular weight: total polymer mass divided by the number of chains

Mw = weight-average molecular weight: average of M weighted by the mass of each chain

Mz = z-average molecular weight: average of M weighted by M times the mass of each chain

Directions

This simulation shows the molecular weight distribution of a polymer and the averages Mn, Mw, and Mz calculated from it.

Choose a distribution type with the buttons at the top left:

  • Schulz–Zimm: set the number-average molecular weight and the dispersity Đ = Mw/Mn (also called the polydispersity index, PDI). Đ = 1 + 1/k, and Đ = 2 is the most-probable distribution.
  • step growth: the Flory most-probable distribution for a linear step-growth polymerization; set the conversion p of functional groups and the repeat-unit molecular weight M0. For step growth, Đ = 1 + p. A conversion of 0.99 gives only 100 repeat units per chain on average.
  • blend of two: mix two Schulz–Zimm polymers; set each component and the mass fraction of component 1. Mn is controlled by the component with more chains; Mw by the component with more mass.

The blue curve is the number distribution (fraction of chains) and the red curve is the weight distribution (fraction of polymer mass), both per decade of molecular weight. In blend of two, the dashed curves show the contribution of each component. The dashed vertical lines mark Mn, Mw, and Mz. Use the checkboxes to show or hide curves.
