Directions

A first- or second-order, irreversible, liquid-phase reaction A → B takes place on a catalyst that loses activity with time on stream. The same mass of catalyst is used in three reactors: a packed-bed reactor (PBR) and a CSTR, in which the catalyst stays in the reactor and deactivates uniformly, and a moving-bed reactor, in which fresh catalyst is fed continuously and spent catalyst is removed.

Select the reaction order (1 or 2) and the deactivation order (0, 1, or 2) and use the sliders to set the Damköhler number and the deactivation rate constant at the reference temperature (500 K), the reactor temperature, and the residence time of the catalyst in the moving bed.

The left plot shows catalyst activity versus catalyst age. In the packed bed and the CSTR, all of the catalyst has been in the reactor since the start, so its age equals the time on stream. In the moving bed, catalyst enters fresh (age 0) and leaves after the catalyst residence time ts, so the bed holds catalyst of all ages from 0 to ts; this range is shaded and marked with an arrow, and the dashed line is the average activity ā of that catalyst. The right plot shows conversion versus time on stream. The moving-bed reactor operates at steady state, so its conversion does not change with time.

Drag on either plot (or use the time-on-stream slider) to move the vertical line; values at that time, and conversions averaged from 0 to that time, are listed below the plots. Use the checkboxes to hide or show reactors. Check T trajectory to replace the conversion plot with the temperature that must be applied, starting from the slider temperature, to hold the packed-bed conversion at its initial value as the catalyst deactivates; raising the temperature this way is the standard industrial response to deactivation, and the run ends when the temperature reaches 600 K. The time axis scales automatically to show the catalyst deactivation.

Details

The catalyst activity \(a\) is the ratio of the reaction rate on used catalyst to the rate on fresh catalyst. The reaction is first order (\(m=1\)) or second order (\(m=2\)) in A:

\[a(t)=\frac{-r_A'(t)}{-r_A'(0)}\]

The activity decays by an \(n\)th-order, concentration-independent (separable) deactivation law:

\[-\frac{da}{dt}=k_d a^n\]

which integrates to

\[n=0:\quad a=1-k_d t \qquad n=1:\quad a=e^{-k_d t} \qquad n=2:\quad a=\frac{1}{1+k_d t}\]

The rate constants depend on temperature through the Arrhenius equation, relative to the reference temperature \(T_0=500\) K:

\[Da=Da_0\exp\!\left[\frac{E}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_0}-\frac{1}{T}\right)\right]\]

with \(E=60\) kJ/mol and \(E_d=120\) kJ/mol. Because \(E_d>E\), raising the temperature increases the initial rate but makes the catalyst deactivate faster. The Damköhler number for fresh catalyst is

\[Da=\frac{k'W C_{A0}^{m-1}}{v_0}\]

where \(W\) is the catalyst mass and \(v_0\) is the volumetric flow rate, and \(C_{A0}\) is the feed concentration of A (the concentration term is absent for \(m=1\)).

Packed bed and CSTR (catalyst stays in the reactor). Deactivation is slow compared with the fluid residence time (the ranges of the sliders keep \(k_d\) below about 0.7 h−1, so the activity half-life is at least about an hour), so the reactor is at pseudo-steady state at each time on stream and the whole bed has the same activity \(a(t)\):

\[\frac{dX}{dW}=\frac{k'aC_{A0}^{m-1}(1-X)^m}{v_0}\]
\[m=1:\quad X=1-\exp(-Da\,a)\]
\[\frac{X}{(1-X)^m}=Da\,a\]
\[m=1:\quad X=\frac{Da\,a(t)}{1+Da\,a(t)}\]

Moving-bed reactor. Fresh catalyst enters with the feed and moves through the bed in plug flow with residence time \(t_s=\frac{W}{U_s}\), where \(U_s\) is the catalyst feed rate (kg/h). Catalyst at position \(W'\) has been in the bed for \(t=W'/U_s\), so its activity is \(a(W'/U_s)\). Integrating the mole balance:

\[\frac{dX}{dW'}=\frac{k'C_{A0}^{m-1}a(1-X)^m}{v_0}\]
\[m=1:\quad X=1-\exp(-Da\,\bar a)\]
\[n=0:\quad \bar a=\begin{cases}1-\frac{k_d t_s}{2}, & k_d t_s\leq1\\[4pt]\frac{1}{2k_d t_s}, & k_d t_s>1\end{cases}\]
\[n=1:\quad \bar a=\frac{1-e^{-k_d t_s}}{k_d t_s}\]

The moving bed operates at steady state, so its conversion does not change with time on stream; a shorter catalyst residence time (faster catalyst feed rate) gives higher activity and conversion, but requires more catalyst regeneration.

Time-averaged conversion. For the PBR and the CSTR, the average conversion from the start of operation to time \(t\) is

\[\bar X(t)=\frac{1}{t}\int_0^t X(\tau)\,d\tau\]

which is proportional to the total amount of product made up to time \(t\).

Temperature trajectory. In industrial practice the reactor temperature is raised as the catalyst deactivates, to hold the conversion constant. For the packed bed, constant conversion requires constant \(Da\,a\), starting from the slider temperature \(T_i\):

\[Da(T)a(t)=Da(T_i)\]

Because the activity now decays at a rising temperature, the deactivation rate constant increases as the activity falls (using \(E_d=2E\)):

\[k_d(T)=k_{d,i}\left[\frac{Da(T)}{Da(T_i)}\right]^{E_d/E}\]

which integrates to

\[a(t)=\left[1-(E_d/E-n+1)k_{d,i}t\right]^{1/(E_d/E-n+1)}\]

The temperature cannot exceed \(T_{\max}=600\) K; the run ends (and the catalyst must be regenerated or replaced) when the temperature reaches \(T_{\max}\):

\[a_{\mathrm{end}}=\exp\!\left[\frac{E}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T_{\max}}-\frac{1}{T_i}\right)\right]\]
