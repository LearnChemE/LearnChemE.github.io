Directions

This simulation shows how a counter-flow, evaporative cooling tower cools hot water by bringing it into direct contact with air. Water trickles downward through a bed of fill while air is drawn upward; a small fraction of the water evaporates, and that evaporation removes most of the heat from the rest of the water. The simulation solves the Merkel equation for the tower's performance.

Use the sliders to set the hot water inlet temperature, the air's dry-bulb and wet-bulb temperatures, the water-to-air mass ratio (L/G), the packing capability (KaV/L), the inlet water flow rate, and the cycles of concentration. The preset buttons load typical operating conditions.

The schematic shows water cooling as it falls through the fill and air warming and picking up moisture as it rises. The cold water leaving the basin is pumped through a heat exchanger, where it absorbs heat from whatever is being cooled, and returns to the top of the tower as the hot water shown entering the fill — the same water is recirculated continuously around this loop. To minimize build up of dissolved solids in the water in the basin, some water is removed in a blowdown stream. Because of blowdown and because some water evaporates, makeup water is added to the basin to maintain a constant flow rate of water through the system. The enthalpy diagram shows the driving force between the air and the saturation curve. The blue line shows the air enthalpy at the bottom and top of the tower but it uses the water temperature at those locations for the x axis. The middle chart shows water and air temperature through the height of the fill. The third chart shows how the cold water temperature depends on L/G at the current packing capability.

Things to try

  • Approach vs. wet bulb. Lower the wet-bulb temperature and watch the cold water follow it down. The cold water can never reach the wet bulb; the gap is the approach.
  • Diminishing returns in fill. Raise KaV/L (more or better packing). The approach shrinks quickly at first, then barely moves. The shaded area between the curves on the enthalpy diagram is what the packing has to “consume.”
  • The L/G trade-off. On the third chart, more air (lower L/G) lowers the cold water temperature, but with diminishing benefit and higher fan power.
  • Humid day. Push the wet bulb toward the dry bulb and watch the air leave the tower saturated with a visible plume.

Details

The tower is modeled with the Merkel equation, which assumes a Lewis factor of 1 and equal heat- and mass-transfer area:

$$\frac{K_aV}{L}=\int_{T_{cold}}^{T_{hot}} \frac{c_L\,dT}{h^*-h}$$

The operating line comes from an energy balance on the air:

$$h = h_{in} + \frac{L}{G}\,c_L\,(T - T_{cold})$$

where $h^*$ is the enthalpy of saturated air at the local water temperature $T$. The simulation finds the value of $T_{cold}$ that makes the Merkel integral equal to the packing capability $K_aV/L$.

Air humidity through the fill is found by integrating

$$\frac{dW}{dx} = W^*-W$$

over the dimensionless height $x$ (0 at the bottom, 1 at the top), and the air dry-bulb temperature follows from the local $h$ and $W$.

Evaporation, blowdown, and makeup water are

$$E = G\,(W_{out}-W_{in}) \qquad B=\frac{E}{COC-1} \qquad \text{makeup} = E + B$$

Terms used in the results

  • Range — $T_{hot}-T_{cold}$, how many degrees the water cools across the tower.
  • Approach — $T_{cold}-$ wet bulb, how close the cold water gets to the coldest temperature that is theoretically possible. A smaller approach means a more effective (and usually larger) tower.
  • Effectiveness — range divided by $(T_{hot}-$ wet bulb$)$, the fraction of the maximum possible cooling that is actually achieved.
  • Dry air flow rate, $G$ — the mass flow rate of the dry air moving through the tower.
  • Evaporation rate, $E$ — the rate at which water is lost to the air as vapor; this is what actually does the cooling.
  • Blowdown, $B$ — water deliberately drained from the basin. As water evaporates it leaves dissolved solids behind, so without blowdown their concentration would keep rising.
  • Makeup — $E+B$, the water that must be added back to replace what is lost to evaporation and blowdown.
  • Cycles of concentration, $COC$ — the ratio of dissolved-solids concentration in the circulating water to that in the makeup water. A higher $COC$ means less blowdown and makeup water, but more scale- and corrosion-forming solids left in the water.
  • Packing capability, $K_aV/L$ — the Merkel number, a dimensionless measure of how effectively the fill transfers heat and mass per unit of water flow. Taller fill, more surface area, or slower water flow all raise it.
  • Fill and packing are two names for the same thing: the material inside the tower that the water trickles over to increase its contact area with the air.

Symbols

  • $K_aV/L$ — Merkel number, the tower's packing capability (dimensionless)
  • $T$, $T_{cold}$, $T_{hot}$ — water temperature, cold water temperature, hot water inlet temperature (°C)
  • $c_L$ — specific heat of liquid water, 4.186 kJ/kg·K
  • $h$ — enthalpy of moist air (kJ/kg dry air)
  • $h^*$ — enthalpy of saturated air at the local water temperature (kJ/kg dry air)
  • $h_{in}$ — enthalpy of the entering air (kJ/kg dry air)
  • $L$ — water mass flow rate (kg/s)
  • $G$ — dry-air mass flow rate (kg/s)
  • $W$, $W^*$ — humidity ratio and saturation humidity ratio (kg water / kg dry air)
  • $x$ — dimensionless height through the fill, 0 at the bottom and 1 at the top
  • $E$ — evaporation rate (kg/s)
  • $B$ — blowdown rate (kg/s)
  • $COC$ — cycles of concentration (dimensionless)
