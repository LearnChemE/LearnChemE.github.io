Directions

This simulation shows concentration polarization: the buildup of rejected solute near a membrane surface caused by convection of feed toward the membrane during filtration.

  • permeate flux — increase it to push more solute toward the membrane. The wall concentration rises and the profile becomes more sharply curved, but the boundary-layer thickness does not change.
  • mass-transfer coefficient — represents cross-flow velocity and turbulence in the feed channel. Increasing it thins the boundary layer (the shaded region), so solute diffuses back into the bulk over a shorter distance and polarization decreases.
  • real rejection — the membrane's intrinsic ability to reject solute. Lower it to see more solute pass into the permeate.
  • The solute feed concentration is set at 4.0 g/L, changing the feed concentration from 1 to 20 g/L does not change the shape of the profile or the boundary layer thickness. The only change is that the scale on the y axis changes.

Watch how the observed rejection (based on measurable bulk and permeate concentrations) falls below the membrane's real rejection as polarization becomes more severe — this is why concentration polarization degrades apparent membrane performance even though the membrane itself hasn't changed.
