Directions

This simulation shows why step-growth polycondensation is limited by chemical equilibrium and why the small-molecule by-product (water) must be removed under vacuum, with devolatilization, to make high-molecular-weight polymer. A diacid and a diol react reversibly to form ester links and water. The equilibrium conversion of functional groups, the number-average degree of polymerization, and the number-average molecular weight are calculated for the selected equilibrium constant, water partial pressure above the melt, and stoichiometric ratio.

Use the sliders to set the equilibrium constant K (typically 1–10 for polyesters and 100–1000 for polyamides), the partial pressure of water in the reactor (the water concentration in the melt is taken to be proportional to this pressure, with no water removed at 1000 mbar), and the stoichiometric ratio r of the two monomers. Choose the polymer to convert the degree of polymerization into a molecular weight, and choose the target degree of polymerization for the dashed line in the plots.

The left plot shows the degree of polymerization at equilibrium versus the water partial pressure; the blue curve is for the selected K, and the grey curves are for other values of K. The black dot is the current condition. The shaded region corresponds to the low pressures that require high vacuum and devolatilization (large melt surface area, thin films, or finishing reactors). When r < 1, the stoichiometric imbalance caps the degree of polymerization regardless of K or pressure, and this cap is shown as a dashed line.

The right plot shows the Carothers equation, which relates degree of polymerization to conversion; the blue dot is the equilibrium conversion for the current conditions. Move the mouse over either plot to read values along the curve.

Details

A diacid (A–A) and a diol (B–B) undergo reversible esterification; every ester link formed releases one water molecule:

\[ \text{–COOH} + \text{HO–} \;\rightleftharpoons\; \text{–COO–} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \]

The stoichiometric ratio \(r \le 1\) is the ratio of the initial concentrations of the two types of functional groups, with A the deficient group. If the initial concentration of B groups is \(M_0\), then the initial concentration of A groups is \(r M_0\). After a fraction \(p\) of the A groups has reacted,

\[ [\text{A}] = r M_0 (1-p) \qquad [\text{B}] = M_0 (1 - r p) \qquad [\text{ester}] = r M_0\, p \]

The amount of water formed equals \(r M_0 p\), but only a fraction \(f\) remains in the melt. The water concentration in the melt is assumed proportional to the partial pressure of water above it (Henry's law), with no water removed at a reference pressure of \(P^\circ = 1000\) mbar:

\[ [\text{H}_2\text{O}] = f\, r M_0\, p \qquad f = \frac{P}{P^\circ} \]

The equilibrium constant, assuming equal reactivity of all functional groups (independent of chain length) and ideal solution behavior, is

\[ K = \frac{[\text{ester}][\text{H}_2\text{O}]}{[\text{A}][\text{B}]} = \frac{f\, r\, p^2}{(1-p)(1-rp)} \]

This is a quadratic equation in \(p\); the root between 0 and 1 is

\[ r(K - f)\, p^2 - K(1+r)\, p + K = 0 \qquad p = \frac{2K}{K(1+r) + \sqrt{K^2(1+r)^2 - 4rK(K-f)}} \]

For \(r = 1\) this reduces to

\[ \frac{p}{1-p} = \sqrt{\frac{K}{f}} \]

The Carothers equation gives the number-average degree of polymerization (number of structural units per chain):

\[ X_n = \frac{1+r}{1+r-2rp} \]

For \(r = 1\), \(X_n = 1/(1-p)\), and combining with the equilibrium expression gives \(X_n = 1 + \sqrt{K/f}\). In a closed reactor with no water removal (\(f = 1\)) and \(K = 4\), \(X_n = 3\), which is only an oligomer. For \(r < 1\), even complete conversion (\(p = 1\)) gives a finite degree of polymerization, the stoichiometric limit:

\[ X_{n,\max} = \frac{1+r}{1-r} \]

The number-average molecular weight is

\[ M_n = X_n\, M_u \]

where \(M_u\) is the mean molar mass of a structural unit (half the repeat unit for an A–A/B–B polymer): 96 g/mol for PET, 110 g/mol for PBT, 113 g/mol for nylon 6,6, and 100 g/mol for the generic polymer.

To reach a target degree of polymerization \(X_t\), the required conversion and the largest fraction of water that can remain are

\[ p_t = \frac{(1+r)(1 - 1/X_t)}{2r} \qquad f_t = \frac{K(1-p_t)(1-rp_t)}{r\, p_t^2} \qquad P_t = f_t\, P^\circ \]

If \(p_t \ge 1\), the target is above the stoichiometric limit and cannot be reached at any pressure. For \(X_t = 100\), \(r = 1\), and \(K = 4\), \(f_t = 4.1\times10^{-4}\), so more than 99.96% of the water must be removed and the water partial pressure must be below about 0.4 mbar. The last stages of polycondensation are therefore run at high vacuum in devolatilizing reactors that continually renew a thin melt surface so water can diffuse out of the viscous polymer.

This simulation considers equilibrium only; reaction kinetics and mass-transfer rates determine how long it takes to approach these values.
