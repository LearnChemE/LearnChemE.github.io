Heat generation and heat removal

Steady-state temperature vs.

Free-radical solution polymerization of styrene or methyl methacrylate (MMA), initiated by AIBN, runs in a jacketed CSTR. The left plot shows the heat generated by polymerization, G(T) (red), and the heat removed by the outflow and the coolant, R(T) (blue), per unit reactor volume. Steady states are the intersections, numbered to match the table below the plots: filled circles are stable and open circles are unstable; a red-outlined open circle is unstable to oscillations.

Change the coolant inlet temperature or coolant flow rate to shift the removal line. Its slope depends on coolant flow and residence time; its intercept depends on coolant temperature. Where the line cuts the S-shaped curve three times, the reactor has two stable steady states.

The right plot is the steady-state reactor temperature versus coolant temperature (or coolant flow). Its coolant-temperature axis normally runs from 280 to 420 K; when the ignition or extinction point lies outside that range, the axis widens to show it. Stable branches are solid green and unstable branches are dashed red. On the dotted red part of the upper branch, the steady state is unstable to oscillations: the reactor temperature cycles instead of settling, so the reactor can fall off the hot branch before reaching the extinction fold. The yellow diamond (on both plots) is the reactor's current state, which follows the dynamic mass and energy balances as you move sliders. Press sweep up to raise the coolant setting steadily to its maximum, and sweep down to lower it to its minimum; pause sweep stops a sweep where it is, and pressing either sweep button continues from there. Starting cold and sweeping up, the reactor ignites at the ignition point; sweeping back down, it stays hot until the extinction point. An orange path records the reactor's temperature during the sweeps, so an up sweep followed by a down sweep traces the hysteresis loop. The path stays on the plot until you move a slider by hand or press another button. When there are multiple steady states, use cold or hot to restart the reactor on the lowest or highest one.

Stability

A steady state where the removal line is less steep than the generation curve () is always unstable (a saddle). Passing the slope test is necessary but not sufficient, so the simulation also evaluates the Jacobian of the three balances at each steady state.
