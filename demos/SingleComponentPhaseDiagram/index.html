Directions

The phase diagram is of a representative fluid. Within the shaded area, the fluid separates into two phases: liquid and gas. The orange line is the critical isotherm. A gas can be liquefied only after it is cooled below its critical temperature. Drag the black dot to vary the pressure and volume of the fluid. The density of a fluid in the piston/cylinder is indicated by its color intensity. Within the shaded two-phase region, the properties of the two phases merge as the critical point is approached.

Details
This simulation uses reduced (dimensionless) properties, defined relative to the fluid's critical pressure pc and critical molar volume vc:
Critical isotherm (T = Tc), shown as the orange curve — the reduced van der Waals equation of state evaluated at the critical temperature:
Coexistence curve (phase boundary), shown as the black curve enclosing the shaded two-phase region:
Saturated liquid and vapor volumes. At a pressure P inside the two-phase region, the coexistence curve gives the reduced volumes of the coexisting saturated liquid, VL, and saturated vapor, VG:
Lever rule. For an overall reduced volume V between VL and VG at pressure P, the liquid and vapor occupy heights of the piston/cylinder found by linear interpolation (the lever rule) between the pure-phase volumes:
Symbols
P – reduced pressure, p/pc
V – reduced volume, v/vc
pc, vc – critical pressure and critical molar volume
Tc – critical temperature
VL, VG – reduced volumes of the saturated liquid and saturated vapor coexisting at pressure P
hL, hG – heights of the liquid and vapor layers shown in the piston/cylinder
