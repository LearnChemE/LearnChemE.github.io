Directions

This simulation compares the steady-state temperature profile along a fin for four tip boundary conditions: tip convection (TC), an adiabatic tip (A), a fixed tip temperature equal to the ambient temperature T∞ (FT), and an infinite fin (IF).

Use the slider bars to adjust the thermal conductivity of the fin k, the convection coefficient h, the ambient temperature T∞, and the ratio of the cross-sectional perimeter Pc to the cross-sectional area Ac. The Pc/Ac slider changes the shape of the cross section shown at the upper right (from a square to a four-pointed star). The base temperature (Tb = 300 K) and fin length (L = 0.17 m) are fixed.

The temperature profiles for each tip condition are shown in the top plot. The total heat dissipated by each fin and the fin effectiveness are shown visually in the two lower bar plots. The use of a fin is generally justified when its effectiveness is above about 2 (dashed line).

Details

The analysis assumes steady-state, one-dimensional conduction along the fin with constant properties and no radiation or volumetric heat generation. Define the excess temperature \(\theta = T - T_\infty\) with base value \(\theta_b = T_b - T_\infty\), the fin parameter \(m\), and the heat-rate scale \(M\):

$$ m=\sqrt{\dfrac{h\,P_c}{k\,A_c}}\,,\qquad M=\sqrt{h\,P_c\,k\,A_c}\;\theta_b $$

Tip convection (TC)

$$ \frac{\theta}{\theta_b}=\frac{\cosh\!\big[m(L-x)\big]+\dfrac{h}{mk}\sinh\!\big[m(L-x)\big]} {\cosh(mL)+\dfrac{h}{mk}\sinh(mL)} $$ $$ q_f=M\,\frac{\sinh(mL)+\dfrac{h}{mk}\cosh(mL)}{\cosh(mL)+\dfrac{h}{mk}\sinh(mL)} $$

Adiabatic tip (A)

$$ \frac{\theta}{\theta_b}=\frac{\cosh\!\big[m(L-x)\big]}{\cosh(mL)}\,,\qquad q_f=M\tanh(mL) $$

Fixed tip temperature at \(T_\infty\) (FT)

$$ \frac{\theta}{\theta_b}=\frac{\sinh\!\big[m(L-x)\big]}{\sinh(mL)}\,,\qquad q_f=M\,\frac{\cosh(mL)}{\sinh(mL)} $$

Infinite fin (IF)

$$ \frac{\theta}{\theta_b}=e^{-mx}\,,\qquad q_f=M $$

The fin effectiveness compares the heat dissipated to that from the bare base area:

$$ \varepsilon_f=\frac{q_f}{h\,A_c\,\theta_b} $$

Fins are typically worthwhile when \(\varepsilon_f \gtrsim 2\). The star-shaped cross section has area \(A_c\) and perimeter \(P_c\); making it spikier increases \(P_c/A_c\).
