Density, Atomic Packing Factor, and Interstitial Sites

Directions

Select Density & APF or Interstitial sites with the buttons at the top right. Drag in the 3D view to rotate the unit cell; press Reset view to return to the starting orientation.

Density & APF: choose a crystal structure and an element, or move the atomic radius and atomic weight sliders to make a custom element. Atoms are colored by where they sit in the unit cell, which sets the fraction of each atom that belongs to the cell. The right panel shows the plane in which the atoms touch; the table below it calculates the lattice parameter, cell volume, atomic packing factor, and density, and compares the density with the measured value.

Interstitial sites: choose an FCC or BCC host, an octahedral or tetrahedral site, and an interstitial atom. Click any site in the 3D view (or press Next site) to show its coordinating host atoms and coordination polyhedron. The right panel is a planar section through the site: the dashed circle is the largest sphere that fits without moving the host atoms, and the filled circle is the interstitial atom.

Hard-sphere model

Atoms are rigid spheres of radius \(R\) that touch along the close-packed direction of each structure. The touching condition gives the lattice parameter \(a\):

$$\text{SC: } a = 2R \qquad \text{BCC: } \sqrt{3}\,a = 4R \qquad \text{FCC: } \sqrt{2}\,a = 4R$$ $$\text{HCP: } a = 2R, \qquad c = \left(\tfrac{c}{a}\right) a, \qquad \left(\tfrac{c}{a}\right)_{\text{ideal}} = \sqrt{8/3} = 1.633$$

Atoms per unit cell

An atom at a cube corner is shared by 8 cells, on an edge by 4, on a face by 2, and inside the cell by 1. In the HCP hexagonal prism, corner atoms are shared by 6 cells.

$$n = N_{\text{inside}} + \tfrac{1}{2}N_{\text{face}} + \tfrac{1}{4}N_{\text{edge}} + \tfrac{1}{8}N_{\text{corner}}$$

This gives \(n\) = 1 (SC), 2 (BCC), 4 (FCC), and 6 (HCP).

Cell volume, packing factor, and density

$$V_C = a^3 \;\;\text{(cubic)} \qquad V_C = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}\,a^2 c \;\;\text{(HCP)}$$ $$\text{APF} = \frac{n\,\tfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3}{V_C} \qquad \rho = \frac{n A}{V_C\,N_A}$$

where \(A\) is the atomic weight (g/mol) and \(N_A = 6.022\times10^{23}\) atoms/mol. The APF is 0.52 for SC, 0.68 for BCC, and 0.74 for FCC and ideal HCP, independent of \(R\). For HCP with a nonideal ratio, \(\text{APF} = 0.7405\,(1.633)/(c/a)\).

Interstitial sites

An interstitial site is a hole between host atoms. The largest sphere of radius \(r\) that fits without displacing the host atoms touches its nearest neighbors, so \(R + r\) equals the distance from the site center to the nearest host-atom center.

$$\text{FCC octahedral: } 2R + 2r = a = 2\sqrt{2}R \;\Rightarrow\; r = (\sqrt{2}-1)R = 0.414R$$ $$\text{FCC tetrahedral: } R + r = \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a \;\Rightarrow\; r = \left(\sqrt{3/2}-1\right)R = 0.225R$$ $$\text{BCC octahedral: } R + r = \tfrac{a}{2} \;\Rightarrow\; r = \left(2/\sqrt{3}-1\right)R = 0.155R$$ $$\text{BCC tetrahedral: } R + r = \tfrac{\sqrt{5}}{4}a \;\Rightarrow\; r = \left(\sqrt{5/3}-1\right)R = 0.291R$$

BCC octahedral sites are distorted: two host atoms are at \(a/2\) and four at \(a/\sqrt{2}\). An interstitial atom pushes only the two close atoms apart, which produces a tetragonal distortion.

Although FCC packs more densely than BCC, its largest hole (octahedral, 0.414\(R\)) is larger than the largest BCC hole (tetrahedral, 0.291\(R\)). This is why carbon (\(r\) ≈ 0.071 nm) is far more soluble in FCC austenite (up to 2.14 wt%) than in BCC ferrite (0.022 wt%).

Atomic radii are hard-sphere values from lattice parameters at room temperature, except γ-Fe, which is stable above 912 °C.
