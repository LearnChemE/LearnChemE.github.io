Directions

This simulation plots the normalized reactant concentration and temperature versus the residence time for a first-order reaction in either an adiabatic CSTR (continuous stirred tank reactor) or an adiabatic PFR (plug-flow reactor). Set the value of −ΔHRCA0/(ρcp) with the slider, where ΔHR is the heat of reaction, ρ is the fluid density, and cp is the fluid heat capacity. For a CSTR, three solutions exist for a range of the residence times; only two solutions are stable.

Details

Consider the first-order chemical reaction A → B taking place either in an adiabatic CSTR or an adiabatic PFR. The rate constant follows the Arrhenius law:

\[ k(T) = 2.6\times10^{20}\exp\!\left(-\dfrac{15000}{T}\right)\ \text{s}^{-1} \]

An adiabatic energy balance links the temperature to the extent of reaction:

\[ T = T_0 + \mathcal{J}\,\dfrac{C_{A0}-C_A}{C_{A0}},\qquad T_0 = 300\ \text{K} \]

where

\[ \mathcal{J} = -\dfrac{\Delta H_R C_{A0}}{\rho c_p}, \]

ΔHR is the heat of reaction, ρ is the fluid density, and cp is the fluid heat capacity. The feed concentration is fixed at CA0 = 1.0, and the concentration axis plots the normalized quantity CA/CA0.

For the adiabatic CSTR, the steady-state design equation relates the residence time τ to the outlet concentration CA implicitly:

\[ \tau = \dfrac{C_{A0}-C_A}{C_A\,k(T(C_A))} \]

This equation is solved parametrically in CA, which reveals that for a range of τ there can be up to three steady-state solutions. A linear stability analysis shows that only two of these three solutions are stable; steady-state multiplicity is physically real for this reactor.

For the adiabatic PFR (equivalent here to a batch reactor followed along its length, with τ playing the role of the space time), the concentration profile is obtained by integrating the ordinary differential equation:

\[ \dfrac{dC_A}{d\tau} = -k(T(C_A))\,C_A,\qquad C_A(0) = C_{A0} \]

Values of the activation energy, pre-exponential factor, and inlet temperature are taken from the original reference. This implementation integrates the PFR equation with a fourth-order Runge–Kutta method and traces the CSTR relation parametrically in CA to reproduce the full multiplicity curve.
