Directions

This simulation plots the normalized reactant concentration CA/CA0 (or the conversion X) and the temperature versus the residence time for a first-order reaction in an adiabatic CSTR (continuous stirred-tank reactor), an adiabatic PFR (plug-flow reactor), or both. Use the sliders to set −ΔHRCA0/(ρcp), the feed temperature, and the residence time, where ΔHR is the heat of reaction, ρ is the fluid density, and cp is the fluid heat capacity. The table below the plot lists the solutions at the selected residence time. Hover over the plot to read values, or click on it to set the residence time.

For a CSTR, three steady states exist over a range of residence times; the middle steady state (dashed) is unstable. Press “hysteresis” to watch the CSTR outlet jump up at ignition as the residence time slowly increases and jump down at extinction as it decreases.

Select “heat balance” to see the CSTR steady states as intersections of the heat-generation and heat-removal curves. Select “dynamics” and click on the plot to choose a starting concentration and temperature; the plot then shows how the CSTR moves to one of its stable steady states.

Details

Consider the first-order chemical reaction A → B taking place in an adiabatic CSTR or an adiabatic PFR. The rate constant follows the Arrhenius law:

\[ k(T) = 2.6\times10^{20}\exp\!\left(-\dfrac{15000}{T}\right)\ \text{min}^{-1} \]

An adiabatic energy balance links the temperature to the conversion:

\[ T = T_0 + \mathcal{J}X,\qquad X = \dfrac{C_{A0}-C_A}{C_{A0}} \]

where

\[ \mathcal{J} = -\dfrac{\Delta H_R C_{A0}}{\rho c_p} \]

The feed concentration is fixed at CA0 = 1.0, and the concentration axis plots the normalized quantity CA/CA0. Because \(T\) is linear in \(X\), the conversion and temperature curves coincide when conversion is plotted.

For the adiabatic CSTR, the steady-state design equation relates the residence time \(\tau\) to the outlet concentration CA implicitly:

\[ \tau = \dfrac{C_{A0}-C_A}{C_A\,k(T(C_A))} \]

This equation is solved parametrically in CA, which reveals that for a range of \(\tau\) there can be three steady-state solutions. A linear stability analysis shows that only two of these three solutions are stable; the middle (unstable) steady state is drawn dashed.

Dividing the CSTR steady-state energy balance by \(\rho c_p/C_{A0}\) expresses the heat generated by reaction and the heat removed by flow in temperature units:

\[ G(T) = \mathcal{J}\,\dfrac{k\tau}{1+k\tau},\qquad R(T) = T - T_0 \]

Steady states are the intersections, \(G = R\). A steady state is stable when \(dR/dT > dG/dT\) at the intersection.

When \(\tau\) is increased slowly, the CSTR stays on the low-conversion branch until ignition, where it jumps to high conversion. When \(\tau\) is then decreased, it stays on the high-conversion branch until extinction, so the path up differs from the path down (hysteresis).

The “dynamics” view integrates the unsteady CSTR mole and energy balances from the starting point you select:

\[ \dfrac{dC_A}{dt} = \dfrac{C_{A0}-C_A}{\tau} - k\,C_A \] \[ \dfrac{dT}{dt} = \dfrac{T_0-T}{\tau} + \mathcal{J}\,k\,\dfrac{C_A}{C_{A0}} \]

All trajectories approach the line \(T = T_0 + \mathcal{J}X\) and then move along it to a stable steady state.

For the adiabatic PFR (a PFR with constant density behaves like a batch reactor, with \(\tau\) in place of batch time), the concentration profile is obtained by integrating the ordinary differential equation:

\[ \dfrac{dC_A}{d\tau} = -k(T(C_A))\,C_A,\qquad C_A(0) = C_{A0} \]

Values of the activation energy and pre-exponential factor are taken from the original reference, which used an inlet temperature of 300 K.

Symbols

\(C_A\) = concentration of A in the reactor outlet
\(C_{A0}\) = feed concentration of A
\(c_p\) = fluid heat capacity
\(G\) = heat generated by reaction, in temperature units (K)
\(\mathcal{J}\) = adiabatic temperature rise for complete conversion (K)
\(k\) = rate constant (min−1)
\(R\) = heat removed by flow, in temperature units (K)
\(t\) = time (min)
\(T\) = temperature (K)
\(T_0\) = inlet temperature (K)
\(X\) = conversion of A
\(\Delta H_R\) = heat of reaction
\(\rho\) = fluid density
\(\tau\) = residence time (min)
