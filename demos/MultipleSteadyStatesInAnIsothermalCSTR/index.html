Directions

An irreversible reaction A → B is carried out in an isothermal, continuous stirred-tank reactor (CSTR). Select a plot with the five gray buttons at the top.

In rate vs. concentration, the green generation curve is the product reaction rate and the blue removal line is the CSTR mass balance. Their intersections are the steady-state solutions; each is marked with a dot (filled = stable, open = unstable) labeled with its CA and conversion X. Drag the residence time slider: when three intersections exist, only two are stable (the middle one is unstable); at high and low residence times only one steady state remains.

CA vs. τ shows every steady-state concentration as a function of residence time. Solid branches are stable and the dashed branch is unstable. The red vertical line marks the current residence time, with dots for its steady states. Three steady states exist only between the two switch points (diamonds) at τ ≈ 1.56 and 2.01 min. The large blue dot is the reactor's operating state: as you drag the residence time slider it stays on its branch until that branch ends at a switch point, then jumps to the other branch (blue arrow). Increasing τ past 2.01 min drops the reactor to the low-concentration branch; it stays there as τ decreases until 1.56 min, where it jumps back up.

In concentration vs. time, conversion vs. time and rate vs. time, also drag the initial reactant concentration slider to set the reactor's starting concentration, then watch how the reactor approaches steady state. When three steady states exist, the concentration and conversion plots show the unstable middle steady state as a gray dashed line; the reactor can never cross it, so it separates the starting values that end at each stable state. The time axis is 25 min, but it lengthens automatically when the reactor needs longer to reach steady state. When three steady states exist, starting above the middle steady-state concentration sends the reactor to the higher-concentration (lower-conversion) steady state; otherwise it settles to the lower-concentration (higher-conversion) steady state.

Details

Rate equations:

τ = V / v ,

rA, g = − 25 CA 2 + CA + 5 CA2 ,

rA, r = ( CA, f − CA ) / τ ,

where τ is residence time (min), V is reactor volume (L), v is the volumetric flow rate (L/min), rA, r is the removal rate (mol/(L·min)) from a mass balance, rA, g is the generation rate (mol/(L·min)) from the reaction kinetics, CA is the concentration of reactant A (mol/L), and CA, f is the feed concentration of A (mol/L).

The transient mass balance solved for each plot is

dCA/dt = ( CA, f − CA ) / τ + rA, g ,   CA(0) = CA, 0 ,

where CA, 0 is the initial concentration of A in the reactor (mol/L). Here the feed concentration CA, f = 6.0 mol/L.

The steady-state curve in the CA vs. τ plot uses the steady-state balance solved for residence time,

τ = ( CA, f − CA ) / rB, g(CA) ,

and the switch points are where dτ/dCA = 0.

Note that rA, g is negative because reactant A is consumed, so the rates in the plots are the rates of product formation, rB, g = −rA, g.
