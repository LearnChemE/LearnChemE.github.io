Directions

Consider an air cooler operating at a constant pressure of 1 atm. Use the sliders to set the temperature and relative humidity hr of the inlet air stream.

This simulation computes the amount of water condensed (in kg) and heat removed (in kJ) after you set the temperature of the cooled outlet air stream. It also gives the fraction of water that is condensed during the cooling process. Note that a basis for the calculation of 1 kg dry air is taken.

In addition, these thermal properties of both the inlet and outlet air streams are listed:

  1. the dry-bulb temperature
  2. the wet-bulb temperature
  3. the dew point temperature
  4. the relative humidity
  5. the absolute humidity or moisture content
  6. the humid volume
  7. the specific enthalpy

Things to try out or check:

  1. For a fixed temperature of the cold outlet air stream, increase the value of the relative humidity of the inlet air stream. As expected, you will observe an increase in the amount of condensed water.
  2. The values of the wet-bulb, dry-bulb and dew point temperatures of the outlet air stream are identical. This is expected because we assume that this stream is saturated (i.e. the relative humidity is 100%).
  3. For fixed temperature and relative humidity values of the inlet air stream, decrease the temperature of the cooled outlet air stream. As expected, you will observe an increase in the amount of heat removed.

Details

Moist air is cooled at a constant total pressure \(P = 1.01325\ \text{bar}\) (1 atm). The saturation pressure of water (bar) is found from an Antoine equation, with temperature \(T\) in \(^\circ\text{C}\):

\[ P_\text{sat} = 10^{\,5.40221 \,-\, 1838.675/(T+241.263)} \]

The moisture content (absolute humidity) \(S_H\) (kg H\(_2\)O per kg dry air) is related to relative humidity \(h_r\) by:

\[ \frac{h_r}{100} \;=\; \frac{S_H\,P}{(0.622 + 0.378\,S_H)\,P_\text{sat}} \]

For the inlet stream, \(S_{H,\text{in}}\) is evaluated at \(T_\text{in}\) and \(h_{r,\text{in}}\). The outlet stream leaves saturated \((h_r = 100\%)\), so \(S_{H,\text{out}}\) is evaluated at \(T_\text{out}\). The water condensed (per kg dry air) and the fraction condensed are:

\[ m = S_{H,\text{in}} - S_{H,\text{out}}, \qquad \text{fraction condensed} = \frac{m}{S_{H,\text{in}}} \]

The specific enthalpy of moist air (kJ per kg dry air, reference \(0\,^\circ\text{C}\)) and of the liquid condensate are:

\[ h = 1.006\,T + S_H\,(1.84\,T + 2501), \qquad h_\ell = 4.1855\,T \]

An energy balance on the cooler gives the heat removed \(Q\) (kJ, per kg dry air):

\[ Q = h_\text{out} + h_\ell\,m - h_\text{in} \]

The humid volume \(v_H\) (m\(^3\) per kg dry air) uses gas constants \(R_a = 286.9\) and \(R_w = 455\ \text{J kg}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\) and \(p = 1.01325\times10^{5}\ \text{Pa}\):

\[ v_H = \frac{(T+273.15)\,(R_a + S_H\,R_w)}{p} \]

The dew point \(T_\text{dp}\) is the saturation temperature at fixed \(S_H\); the wet-bulb temperature \(T_\text{wb}\) is found by requiring a saturated state of equal enthalpy:

\[ P_\text{sat}(T_\text{dp}) = \frac{S_H\,P}{0.622 + 0.378\,S_H}, \qquad h\big(T_\text{wb},\,S_H^{\text{sat}}(T_\text{wb})\big) = h_\text{stream} \]

Reference
[1] R. M. Felder and R. W. Rousseau, Elementary Principles of Chemical Processes, 3rd ed., New York: John Wiley & Sons, 2005.
