Directions

This simulation shows how the rate of initiation, relative to the rate of propagation, affects the chain-length distribution and the polydispersity index (PDI) of a polymer made by living anionic polymerization in a batch reactor. Because there is no termination or chain transfer, fast initiation produces a narrow Poisson distribution with PDI ≈ 1 + 1/Xn. When initiation is slow, chains start at different times, some initiator is still unreacted when the monomer is consumed, and the distribution broadens.

Set the ratio of initial monomer to initiator concentrations [M]0/[I]0 and the ratio of initiation to propagation rate constants ki/kp (log scale). The fast initiation and slow initiation buttons set ki/kp to 100 and 0.01. Drag the monomer conversion slider, or press Play to follow the polymerization from low conversion to complete conversion. Select number fraction or weight fraction for the distribution plot.

The left plot compares the chain-length distribution (red) with the Poisson distribution for instantaneous initiation at the same monomer conversion (black, dashed). The right plot shows the polydispersity index versus monomer conversion for the same two cases.
