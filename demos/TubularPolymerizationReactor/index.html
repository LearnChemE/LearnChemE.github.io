flow model

This simulation models bulk free-radical polymerization of styrene in a 20 m jacketed tube. Monomer with dissolved initiator enters at the feed temperature, and the tube wall is held at the wall temperature. The temperature changes with radial and axial locations so the conversion and viscosity also depend on both radial and axial locations. The profiles also depend on whether plug flow, laminar flow, or laminar with viscosity rise is selected.

Select a flow model. Plug flow gives every fluid element the same residence time. Laminar, Newtonian keeps the parabolic velocity profile. Laminar, viscosity rise recalculates the velocity profile at each axial position from the local viscosity, so slow, viscous fluid near the wall pushes more of the flow into the center.

The plots show axial profiles of conversion, temperature and viscosity along the centerline, along the outer stream (the stream tube that enters at 90% of the radius), and for the mixing-cup average. The bottom row shows radial velocity profiles, the cumulative residence-time distribution, and the molecular-weight distribution of the polymer leaving the tube.

Increase the tube diameter or the wall temperature to see the hot spot grow into a thermal runaway. Increase the viscosity-rise parameter to see channeling in the core.

Kinetics (bulk styrene, initiated plus thermal)

\[R_i = 2 f k_d[\mathrm{I}] + 2k_{th}[\mathrm{M}]^3 \qquad [\mathrm{P}^\bullet]=\sqrt{\frac{R_i}{2k_t}} \qquad R_p = k_p[\mathrm{M}][\mathrm{P}^\bullet]\]

The gel effect lowers the termination rate constant as conversion \(X\) increases (Hui and Hamielec):

\[k_t = k_{t0}\exp\!\left[-2\left(A_1X+A_2X^2+A_3X^3\right)\right]\] \[A_1 = 2.57-5.05\times10^{-3}T,\quad A_2=9.56-1.76\times10^{-2}T,\quad A_3=-3.03+7.85\times10^{-3}T\]
\(k_d = 6.38\times10^{13}\,e^{-14842/T}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\)initiator (benzoyl-peroxide-like), \(f=0.6\)
\(k_p = 1.051\times10^{7}\,e^{-3557/T}\ \mathrm{L/(mol\,s)}\)propagation
\(k_{t0} = 1.255\times10^{9}\,e^{-844/T}\ \mathrm{L/(mol\,s)}\)termination by combination
\(k_{th} = 2.19\times10^{5}\,e^{-13810/T}\ \mathrm{L^2/(mol^2 s)}\)thermal initiation
\(C_M = 0.2198\,e^{-2820/T}\)chain transfer to monomer

Stream tubes

Radial diffusion of polymer is negligible, so the tube is divided into 40 annular stream tubes. Each tube \(i\) carries a fixed volumetric flow \(q_i\) set by the inlet velocity profile. Along each tube, with \(dt = dz/\bar u_i\):

\[\frac{dX}{dt} = k_p[\mathrm{P}^\bullet](1-X) \qquad \frac{d[\mathrm{I}]}{dt} = -k_d[\mathrm{I}]\]

Energy balance

Heat moves between tubes by radial conduction, and the wall is held at \(T_w\):

\[\rho c_p u\frac{\partial T}{\partial z} = \frac{k}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r\frac{\partial T}{\partial r}\right) + (-\Delta H_p)R_p \qquad T(R,z)=T_w\]

Conduction is solved implicitly at each axial step. The adiabatic temperature rise for full conversion is \(\Delta T_{ad} = (-\Delta H_p)[\mathrm{M}]_0/(\rho c_p) \approx 356\ \mathrm{K}\), which is why hot spots grow quickly in wide tubes.

Velocity profile

For slowly varying viscosity (lubrication approximation), with \(G=-dp/dz\):

\[u(r) = \frac{G}{2}\int_r^R \frac{r'}{\mu(r')}\,dr' \qquad Q = \int_0^R 2\pi r\,u\,dr\]

In the viscosity-rise model, \(G\) and the tube boundaries are found at each \(z\) so every stream tube still carries its own \(q_i\). Viscous wall tubes widen and slow down, and the core speeds up. The pressure drop is \(\Delta p = \int_0^L G\,dz\).

Viscosity

\[\mu = \mu_m \exp\!\left[\frac{B X}{1+0.5X} + (1800+7000X)\left(\frac{1}{T}-\frac{1}{373\ \mathrm{K}}\right)\right] \qquad \mu_m = 0.35\ \mathrm{mPa\,s}\]

\(B\) lumps the effect of polymer concentration and chain length on viscosity. This is an illustrative correlation for teaching.

Residence-time distribution

\[F(t) = \sum_{t_i\le t}\frac{q_i}{Q} \qquad \text{laminar, Newtonian: } F(\theta) = 1-\frac{1}{4\theta^2},\ \theta=\frac{t}{\tau}\ge\frac12\]

Molecular-weight distribution

Polymer made at each instant follows a Schulz distribution with number-average degree of polymerization

\[\overline{DP}_{n,\mathrm{inst}} = \frac{R_p}{R_i/2 + C_M R_p}\]

and shape parameter between 1 (all transfer) and 2 (all combination). The outlet distribution, plotted as weight fraction per decade \(W(\log M)\), is the sum over every axial step and stream tube, weighted by the polymer flow in each tube.

Physical properties

\(\rho = 900\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}\), \(c_p = 1.9\ \mathrm{kJ/(kg\,K)}\), \(k = 0.13\ \mathrm{W/(m\,K)}\), \(\Delta H_p = -69.9\ \mathrm{kJ/mol}\), \([\mathrm{M}]_0 = 8.7\ \mathrm{mol/L}\), \(M_{\mathrm{styrene}} = 104.15\ \mathrm{g/mol}\), \(L = 20\ \mathrm m\).
