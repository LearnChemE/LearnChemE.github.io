Directions

This simulation shows dopant concentration versus depth in a silicon wafer for the two-step diffusion process used to form p–n junctions. In the first step (predeposition), the surface is held at a constant dopant concentration Cs, which gives a complementary error function (erfc) profile. In the second step (drive-in), the dopant source is removed and the fixed amount of dopant introduced during predeposition diffuses deeper into the wafer, which gives an approximately Gaussian profile.

Select the dopant (boron or phosphorus) and set the temperature and time for each step with the sliders. The wafer has a uniform background dopant concentration CB of the opposite type. The junction depth xj is where the diffused dopant concentration equals CB; it is marked on the plot with a dashed vertical line.

Check "exact drive-in" to compare the Gaussian approximation with the exact solution for diffusion from the predeposited erfc profile. The Gaussian is accurate when the drive-in diffusion length D2t2 is much larger than the predeposition diffusion length D1t1. Concentration is plotted on a log scale.
