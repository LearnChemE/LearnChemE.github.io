Directions

This simulation applies Flory–Huggins lattice theory to a binary mixture of A and B, where A is usually a polymer with NA segments and B is a solvent (NB = 1) or a second polymer (NB > 1). Each segment and each solvent molecule occupies one lattice site; φ is the volume fraction of A.

The left plot shows the Gibbs free energy of mixing per lattice site, divided by RT, at the selected temperature. Each curve is labeled directly on the plot. The curve is blue where a single phase is stable, orange where it is metastable, and red where it is unstable. When two phases form, the green dashed line is the common tangent, and its points of tangency (open circles) are the compositions of the coexisting phases. The red diamonds mark the spinodal points, the inflection points where the curvature of the free-energy curve changes sign; between them the curve is concave down and a single phase is unstable.

The right plot is the phase diagram. The binodal (blue) encloses the two-phase region; the spinodal (red dashed) encloses the unstable region. The bright green point is the critical point, where the binodal and spinodal meet. When two phases form, the green horizontal tie line connects the compositions of the coexisting phases (open circles). The black point is the overall composition φ0 at temperature T; drag it, or drag along the free-energy curve, to change φ0 and T. Use the buttons above the phase diagram to plot temperature or the interaction parameter on the vertical axis.

The interaction parameter depends on temperature as = α + β/T, where α is the temperature-independent (entropic) part and β is the enthalpic coefficient. With β > 0 the mixture has an upper critical solution temperature (UCST); with β < 0 it has a lower critical solution temperature (LCST). The NA and NB sliders (lattice sites per molecule) are logarithmic, 1 to 1000.
