Henry's function f(κa)

Directions

Two charged spheres (A and B) sit in an electrolyte solution between two electrodes. Each migrates toward the electrode of opposite sign, at a speed set by its size, its surface (zeta) potential, the field strength, and how compressed its surrounding ion cloud is.

Use the sliders to change the electric field, the ionic strength of the solution, and each particle's radius and zeta potential. Watch:

  • Which electrode each particle heads toward — set by the sign of its zeta potential.
  • How its speed changes with field strength, size, and zeta potential.
  • Where each particle's κa falls on the f(κa) curve at right — near κa → 0 it behaves by the Hückel limit; at large κa it approaches the Smoluchowski limit.

The channel animation is sped up for clarity; the real, computed velocity and crossing time for a 1 cm gap are reported below the animation.

Details

A charged particle in an electric field E moves at a terminal velocity v = μE, where μ is the electrophoretic mobility. For a sphere of radius a with zeta potential ζ, immersed in an electrolyte of permittivity ε and viscosity η, Henry's equation gives:

$$\mu = \dfrac{2\,\varepsilon\,\zeta}{3\,\eta}\,f(\kappa a)$$

where κ is the inverse Debye length, so κ−1 is the Debye length — the thickness of the diffuse ion cloud around the particle — and the dimensionless group κa compares the particle's radius a to that ion-cloud thickness. f(κa) is Henry's function, which interpolates between two limits:

  • Hückel limit (κa « 1, thick double layer — small particles or dilute solution): f → 1, so $\mu = \tfrac{2}{3}\varepsilon\zeta/\eta$.
  • Smoluchowski limit (κa » 1, thin double layer — large particles or concentrated solution): f → 1.5, so $\mu = \varepsilon\zeta/\eta$.

This simulation uses Ohshima's closed-form approximation to Henry's function:

$$f(\kappa a) = 1 + \dfrac{1}{2\left[\,1 + \dfrac{2.5}{\kappa a\left(1+2e^{-\kappa a}\right)}\,\right]^{3}}$$

The Debye length is computed from the ionic strength I (mol/L) of a 1:1 electrolyte in water at 25°C:

$$\kappa^{-1}\ (\text{nm}) = \dfrac{0.304}{\sqrt{I}}$$

Fixed properties used: water at 25°C, ε = εrε0 = 78.5 × 8.854×10−12 F/m, η = 8.9×10−4 Pa·s.

Direction: the force on the particle is F = qE, so a particle with negative ζ migrates toward the anode (+) and one with positive ζ migrates toward the cathode (−) — the same convention that carries DNA and most proteins toward the positive electrode in gel electrophoresis.
