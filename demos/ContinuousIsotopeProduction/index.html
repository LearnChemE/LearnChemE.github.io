production → isotope 1 → isotope 2 → isotope 3 → isotope 4 (stable)

Directions

Isotope 1 is continuously produced at a constant rate of 30 particles/min, and it then undergoes radioactive decay. The decay products build up and, if radioactive, later decay with time. In this simulation isotope 1 decays to isotope 2, which decays to isotope 3, which decays to the stable isotope 4. The half-lives of the three decay processes are selected with the sliders. The numbers of particles of isotopes 1, 2 and 3 approach steady-state values, which are shown as dashed lines in the same colors as the curves. Because isotope 4 is stable, the number of isotope 4 particles continuously increases with time; by default it is not shown in the graph so that the behavior of the other isotopes can be more readily observed, but it can be added to the plot with the isotope 4 checkbox.

Select the Example button to see the decay chain for 211Pb, continuously produced at 20 particles per minute. In nature, 211Pb is formed in the actinium decay series from the long-lived 227Ac (half-life 21.8 years) through a sequence of decays that ends with the short-lived 219Rn and 215Po, so over a few hours its production rate is essentially constant.

Details

The number of particles of each isotope is found by numerically integrating the coupled first-order rate equations (RK4), starting from zero particles of isotope 1:

$$\frac{dN_1}{dt} = P - \lambda_1 N_1$$

\(P\) = production rate of isotope 1

$$\frac{dN_2}{dt} = \lambda_1 N_1 - \lambda_2 N_2$$
$$\frac{dN_3}{dt} = \lambda_2 N_2 - \lambda_3 N_3$$
$$\frac{dN_4}{dt} = \lambda_3 N_3$$

The decay constant of each step is obtained from its half-life:

$$\lambda_i = \frac{\ln 2}{(t_{1/2})_i}$$

The dashed lines show the steady-state numbers of particles of isotopes 1, 2 and 3, found by setting \(dN_i/dt = 0\):

$$N_{i,ss} = \frac{P}{\lambda_i}$$

Because isotope 1 is continuously produced and isotope 4 is stable, the total \(N_1+N_2+N_3+N_4\) increases with time.

In the 211Pb example, all 211Bi is assumed to undergo α decay to 207Tl; the minor branch (about 0.3%) in which 211Bi undergoes β− decay to 211Po, which then α-decays to 207Pb, is neglected.
