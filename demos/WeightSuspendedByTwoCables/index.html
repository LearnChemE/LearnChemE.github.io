Directions

This Demonstration calculates the tension forces in two cables that support a weight. Select equal cable lengths and the tension is the same in each cable; the tension changes as a slider changes the angle between the cables. Select unequal cable lengths and the tension in the cables is different. Changing the angle of each cable to the horizontal with a slider changes the cable length. Change the mass of the ball with a slider.

Details

For equal cable lengths, a force balance in the \(y\) direction is done to determine the cable tension:

\[\sum F_y = 0 = 2\,T \sin(90^\circ - \theta/2) - m\,g.\]

Rearranging to solve for tension:

\[T = \frac{m\,g}{2 \sin(90^\circ - \theta/2)}.\]

When the cable lengths are unequal, force balances in the \(x\) and \(y\) directions are done to calculate the tension in each cable:

\[\sum F_y = 0 = T_1 \sin\theta_1 + T_2 \sin\theta_2 - m\,g,\]

\[\sum F_x = 0 = -T_1 \cos\theta_1 + T_2 \cos\theta_2.\]

Solving these two equations simultaneously:

\[T_1 = \frac{m\,g\cos\theta_2}{\sin(\theta_1 + \theta_2)}, \qquad T_2 = \frac{m\,g\cos\theta_1}{\sin(\theta_1 + \theta_2)}.\]

Symbols

\(F_x, F_y\) = forces in the horizontal and vertical directions (N)

\(T\) = tension in each cable when the cables are equal length (N)

\(T_1, T_2\) = tensions in the left and right cables (N)

\(\theta\) = angle between the two cables (degrees)

\(\theta_1, \theta_2\) = angles of the left and right cables from the horizontal (degrees)

\(m\) = mass of the ball (kg)

\(g\) = acceleration due to gravity, 9.8 m/s\(^2\)
