Directions

This simulation shows a calculation of heat transfer through a single fin (either a rectangular or a pin fin) mounted on a heat sink. Use the sliders to vary the fin length and the ambient air temperature. Select the fin material from the drop-down menu. The temperature distribution is shown on the fin surface using a color scale (red is hottest, blue is coolest), and the temperature of the fin tip is shown on the right along with the temperature legend. The thickness of the rectangular fin and the diameter of the pin fin are both 1.5 mm. The heat transfer rate through the fin is calculated and displayed above the fin, as well as the fin effectiveness—the ratio of the fin heat transfer rate to the heat transfer rate without the fin. The fin tip is assumed to be adiabatic and the air flow laminar.

Details

The heat transfer coefficient of air \(h\) is calculated using Nusselt correlations for laminar flow over a flat plate and a cylinder. Air properties are evaluated at 300 K. The average Nusselt and Reynolds numbers are:

\(\mathrm{Nu}_{\mathrm{rect}} = \dfrac{h\,w}{k_a}\), \(\;\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{rect}} = \dfrac{u\,w}{\nu}\) for the rectangular fin of width \(w\),

\(\mathrm{Nu}_{\mathrm{pin}} = \dfrac{h\,t}{k_a}\), \(\;\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{pin}} = \dfrac{u\,t}{\nu}\) for the pin fin of diameter \(t\).

For a rectangular fin:

\(\mathrm{Nu}_{\mathrm{rect}} = 0.664\,\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{rect}}^{1/2}\,\mathrm{Pr}^{1/3}\),

for a pin fin:

\(\mathrm{Nu}_{\mathrm{pin}} = 0.683\,\mathrm{Re}_{\mathrm{pin}}^{0.466}\,\mathrm{Pr}^{1/3}\),

where the Prandtl number is:

\(\mathrm{Pr} = \dfrac{C_p\,\mu}{k_a}\).

For a fin with an adiabatic tip:

\(T = T_{\infty} + \dfrac{\cosh(m\,(L - z))}{\cosh(m\,L)}\,(T_b - T_{\infty})\),

\(q_f = \sqrt{h\,P\,k\,A_c}\;\tanh(m\,L)\,(T_b - T_{\infty})\),

\(m = \sqrt{\dfrac{h\,P}{k\,A_c}}\).

For a rectangular fin:

\(P = 2\,w + 2\,t\),

\(A_c = w\,t\).

For a pin fin:

\(P = \pi\,t\),

\(A_c = \dfrac{\pi}{4}\,t^2\).

The fin effectiveness is the ratio of the fin heat transfer rate to the heat transfer rate that would exist without the fin:

\(\varepsilon_f = \dfrac{q_f}{h\,A_c\,(T_b - T_{\infty})}\).

The thermal conductivities (\(\mathrm{W/m\ K}\)) of the fin materials are:

stainless steel\(k = 14\)
carbon steel\(k = 60.5\)
iron\(k = 80.2\)
brass\(k = 110\)
aluminum\(k = 237\)
copper\(k = 401\)
