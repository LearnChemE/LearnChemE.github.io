Directions

This simulation models bulk free-radical polymerization of methyl methacrylate (MMA) with AIBN initiator in a jacketed, well-mixed batch reactor. The reactor starts at the jacket temperature with no polymer.

Set the initiator loading, jacket temperature and heat-removal capacity with the sliders. The left plot shows reactor temperature (red) and monomer conversion (blue) for 300 minutes. The dashed orange line is the maximum allowable temperature of 100 °C, close to the normal boiling point of MMA. A run is labeled runaway if the reactor temperature exceeds it.

Uncheck gel effect to see how much of the runaway comes from the drop in the termination rate constant as the mixture becomes viscous. Check loss of cooling to cut the heat-removal capacity at a chosen time to a chosen fraction of its normal value; 0% means the reactor becomes adiabatic.

The right plot has three views:

Semenov diagram: heat generation Qg versus temperature at the composition the reactor has at the selected time, with the heat-removal line Qr. Drag on the left plot or use the time slider to move through the batch. The dotted green line (critical Qr) is the critical removal line tangent to Qg; when Qr is less steep than it, there is no low-temperature intersection and the temperature must climb.

Semenov number: Ψ versus time, compared with the classical critical value 1/e.

Safe-limit scan: the peak temperature for a range of initiator loadings or jacket temperatures, with the other settings fixed. It reports the maximum safe value from the simulation (peak temperature = 100 °C) and from the Semenov criterion (largest Ψ = 1/e). Find the maximum safe initiator loading or jacket temperature and compare the two estimates.

Details

Kinetics

Initiator decomposition, propagation and termination:

$$\mathrm{I} \xrightarrow{k_d} 2\,\mathrm{R}^\bullet \ (\text{efficiency } f), \qquad \mathrm{P}_n^\bullet + \mathrm{M} \xrightarrow{k_p} \mathrm{P}_{n+1}^\bullet, \qquad \mathrm{P}_n^\bullet + \mathrm{P}_m^\bullet \xrightarrow{k_t} \text{dead polymer}$$

With the quasi-steady-state approximation for radicals, the total radical concentration is

$$\lambda_0 = \sqrt{\frac{2 f k_d [\mathrm{I}]}{k_t}}$$

Mole and energy balances

$$\frac{d[\mathrm{I}]}{dt} = -k_d [\mathrm{I}]$$ $$\frac{dX}{dt} = k_p (1-X)\,\lambda_0$$ $$\rho c_p \frac{dT}{dt} = \underbrace{(-\Delta H_p)[\mathrm{M}]_0 \frac{dX}{dt}}_{Q_g} \; - \; \underbrace{\frac{UA}{V}\,(T - T_j)}_{Q_r}$$

where X is monomer conversion, [M]0 = 9.4 mol/L (bulk MMA), −ΔHp = 57.8 kJ/mol and ρcp = 1800 kJ/(m³·K). The adiabatic temperature rise for complete conversion is about 300 K. After cooling is lost at time tf, UA/V is multiplied by the remaining fraction.

Gel (Trommsdorff) effect

As polymer accumulates, the viscosity rises and the large radicals can no longer diffuse together to terminate. The Schmidt–Ray correlation for MMA reduces kt with conversion (T in K):

$$k_t = k_{t0}\, g_t, \qquad g_t = \exp\!\left[-2\left(A_1 X + A_2 X^2 + A_3 X^3\right)\right]$$ $$A_1 = 2.57 - 5.05\times10^{-3}\,T, \quad A_2 = 9.56 - 1.76\times10^{-2}\,T, \quad A_3 = -3.03 + 7.85\times10^{-3}\,T$$

Because the rate is proportional to kt−1/2, the rate accelerates as conversion increases, even at constant temperature. This autoacceleration and the Arrhenius rise of the rate with temperature reinforce each other.

Semenov analysis

At any instant, the heat generation rate depends on temperature through the rate constants, with composition (X, [I]) fixed:

$$Q_g(T) = (-\Delta H_p)[\mathrm{M}]_0\, k_p(T)\,(1-X)\sqrt{\frac{2 f k_d(T) [\mathrm{I}]}{k_{t0}(T)\, g_t(X,T)}}, \qquad Q_r(T) = \frac{UA}{V}(T - T_j)$$

A stable operating point needs an intersection where the removal line is steeper than the generation curve. The critical removal line passes through (Tj, 0) and is tangent to Qg; its slope is the minimum over T > Tj of Qg(T)/(T − Tj).

Approximating the rate with one apparent activation energy gives the Semenov number and the classical critical value:

$$\Psi = \frac{Q_g(T_j)}{UA/V}\,\frac{E_{app}}{R T_j^2} \le \frac{1}{e}, \qquad E_{app} = E_p + \frac{E_d}{2} - \frac{E_t}{2} = 81.2\ \text{kJ/mol}$$

Ψ is evaluated at the jacket temperature and the current composition. The gel effect raises Ψ during the batch. The Semenov criterion neglects reactant consumption, so it is conservative here: the batch can pass Ψ = 1/e and still finish below the allowable temperature if monomer or initiator is used up first.

Safe-limit scan

The scan runs the full model across the slider range of the chosen variable. The simulation limit is where the peak temperature reaches 100 °C. The Semenov limit is where the largest Ψ during normal operation (full cooling) reaches 1/e. The sharp rise of peak temperature with the scanned variable is the parametric sensitivity that marks runaway.

Assumptions

Well-mixed, constant volume (density change on polymerization is neglected), constant ρcp and f, no glass effect on kp, no depropagation (the ceiling temperature of PMMA is near 220 °C), and no boiling or pressure rise. Temperatures above about 100 °C would boil MMA in an open reactor, so the curves beyond that point show the thermal driving force, not a realistic trajectory.
