Directions

This simulation shows the velocity and temperature profiles of two liquids at different initial temperatures flowing concurrently in laminar flow in a cylindrical tube and the surrounding cylindrical annulus.

The properties of the two liquids are assumed to be constant, and their velocity profiles are assumed to be independent of axial location. In the inlet region \(z < 0\), the liquids in the annular and the tube regions have different uniform temperatures \(T_{\text{annulus}}\) and \(T_{\text{tube}}\), respectively; in the region \(z > 0\), the external annulus wall is thermally insulated and the wall of the circular tube is assumed to be thermally thin.

Similar arrangements occur in various industrial applications, including heat exchangers and tubular reactors. You can vary the ratio of the properties of the fluid in the cylinder to the fluid in the annulus, the relative dimension of the tubular to axial radii and the pressure gradient in the two flow regions to see the temperature and velocities in the two flow regions.

Use the drop-down menu to select the plot to display: the average (cup) temperature of each fluid along the tube, the radial temperature profile at a selected axial distance, the fluid velocity profiles, or the temperature contours.

Details

The dimensionless horizontal distance is

$$z = \frac{z^{*}}{L},$$

where \(L\) is the length of the tube. The dimensionless radial distance is

$$r = \frac{r^{*}}{R},$$

where \(R\) is the external annular radius, and the dimensionless temperature is

$$\frac{T(z,r) - T_{\text{tube}}(0,r)}{T_{\text{annulus}}(0,r) - T_{\text{tube}}(0,r)}.$$

The temperature profile of the two liquids is obtained by solving a partial differential equation with two different sets of parameters and velocity profiles, one for each flow region:

$$u\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial z} \;=\; \alpha\,\frac{1}{r}\, \frac{\partial}{\partial r}\!\left(r\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial r}\right) \;+\; \frac{1}{\mathrm{Pe}}\,\frac{\partial^{2} T}{\partial z^{2}},$$

here:

$$T=\begin{cases} T_{\text{tube}} & r\le \kappa R\\[2pt] T_{\text{annulus}} & \kappa R < r < R \end{cases} \qquad u=\begin{cases} u_{\text{tube}} & r\le \kappa R\\[2pt] u_{\text{annulus}} & \kappa R < r < R \end{cases}$$ $$\alpha=\begin{cases} \alpha_{\text{tube}} & r\le \kappa R\\[2pt] \alpha_{\text{annulus}} & \kappa R < r < R \end{cases} \qquad \mathrm{Pe}=\begin{cases} \mathrm{Pe}_{\text{tube}} & r\le \kappa R\\[2pt] \mathrm{Pe}_{\text{annulus}} & \kappa R < r < R \end{cases}$$

where \(\kappa\) is the ratio of the radii of the tube to the annulus, \(T\) is the dimensionless temperature, \(u\) is the velocity, \(\mathrm{Pe}\) is the Péclet number \(u L/\alpha\), \(\alpha\) is the thermal diffusivity \(k/(\rho\,C_p)\), and \(k\), \(\rho\) and \(C_p\) are the fluid thermal conductivity, density and heat capacity, respectively. The initial conditions are:

$$T_{\text{tube}}(0,r)=0, \qquad T_{\text{annulus}}(0,r)=1,$$

with boundary conditions

$$\frac{\partial T}{\partial r}(R,z)=\frac{\partial T}{\partial r}(-R,z)=0.$$

Here \(\kappa R\) and \(R\) are the radii of the tube and the annulus respectively.

Analytic solutions for fully developed laminar flow in the tube and the cylinder are shown in [1]:

$$u_{\text{tube}}=\frac{\Delta P_{\text{tube}}\,(\kappa R)^{2}} {4\,\mu_{\text{tube}}\,L}\left(1-\left(\frac{r}{\kappa R}\right)^{\!2}\right),$$ $$u_{\text{annulus}}=\frac{\Delta P_{\text{annulus}}\,R^{2}} {4\,\mu_{\text{annulus}}\,L}\left[\left(1-\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\!2}\right) -\frac{1-\kappa^{2}}{\ln(1/\kappa)}\,\ln\!\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)\right],$$

and the maximum velocity is

$$U_{\max}=\begin{cases} U_{\text{tube}}=\dfrac{\Delta P_{\text{tube}}\,(\kappa R)^{2}} {4\,\mu_{\text{tube}}\,L} & r\le \kappa R\\[14pt] U_{\text{annulus}}=\dfrac{\Delta P_{\text{annulus}}\,R^{2}} {4\,\mu_{\text{annulus}}\,L}\left(1-\lambda^{2} \left(1-\ln \lambda^{2}\right)\right) & \kappa R \le r < R \end{cases}$$

Here \(\Delta P\) is the horizontal pressure and

$$\lambda=\sqrt{\frac{1-\kappa^{2}}{2\,\ln(1/\kappa)}}.$$

The average (cup) temperatures of the two fluids are:

$$\tilde{T}_{\text{tube}}=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\kappa R} T\,u_{\text{tube}}\,dr} {\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\kappa R} u_{\text{tube}}\,dr}, \qquad \tilde{T}_{\text{annular}}=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\kappa R}^{R} T\,u_{\text{annulus}}\,dr} {\displaystyle\int_{\kappa R}^{R} u_{\text{annulus}}\,dr}.$$

These equations are solved numerically. Plots of the temperature and velocity contours, as well as the average temperatures, are shown.

Reference

[1] R. B. Bird, W. E. Stewart and E. N. Lightfoot, Transport Phenomena, 2nd ed., New York: John Wiley and Sons, 2002.
