One- and two-compartment pharmacokinetic models calculate the concentration of a drug in the body as a function of time for a given dosing scheme. There are two options for administering the drug: injection into compartment 1 (e.g., inject into the blood stream with a needle) or absorption into compartment 1 from compartment 2 (e.g., drug in stomach absorbed into blood stream). Select the mass of the dose, number of doses and intervals between doses with sliders. The objective is to select a dosing scheme so that as much of the drug as possible has a concentration between the effective dose and the toxic dose (the therapeutic window). The drug can be eliminated from compartment 1 (set rate constant ke,1 with a slider) or transferred to compartment 2 (slider k12). Similarly, in compartment 2 the drug can be eliminated (slider ke,2) or transferred back to compartment 1 (slider k21). Change the reaction order of the drug elimination kinetics with buttons. The areas under the curves correspond to the total exposure to the drug.

The drug is well-mixed in either compartment. The drug is either administered as a rapid dose (i.e., bolus injection) or it absorbs into compartment 1. The constants k12 and k21 represent the rate transfer coefficients from compartment 1 to compartment 2 and vice versa, assuming a first-order transfer between them. Each compartment has its own elimination rate ke,1 and ke,2. The absorption coefficient ka determines the rate at which the drug absorbs into compartment 1, and it assumes first-order absorption in the amount of the dose. The concentrations and time are intentionally left unit-less, but the system is dimensionally consistent. The drug elimination reaction order is ro.

When the dose absorbs into compartment 1, then the amount changes with time:

d(dose)/dt = −ka (dose)

If there is no absorption and the dose is directly injected, then the dose amount is distributed within compartment 1 at the injection time. The first dose is at time zero, and the same mass is then injected for each subsequent dose.

Compartment 1:

dC1/dt = ka(dose/V) − k12C1 + k21C2 − ke,1C1ro

where C1 is the drug concentration in compartment 1, C2 is the drug concentration in compartment 2, and V is the volume of compartments 1 and 2.

Initial condition for compartment 1: C1 = 0 when absorption occurs; C1 = (mass of dose)/V when absorption does not occur.

Compartment 2:

dC2/dt = k12C1 − k21C2 − ke,2C2ro

with initial condition C2 = 0.
