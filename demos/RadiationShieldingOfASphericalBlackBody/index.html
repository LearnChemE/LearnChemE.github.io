Directions

This simulation shows how a radiation shield affects radiative heat transfer to a ball. The ball, in the center of an enclosure, is the object that is shielded; you can remove the shield by unchecking "shield". The enclosure around the shield and ball is assumed to be a black body at a temperature of 600 K. The radiation shield diameter is 0.2 m larger than the ball diameter, and the shield is thin enough that conductive heat transfer can be ignored. Use buttons to view a radiation network or a plot of the radiative heat transfer from the enclosure to the ball as a function of the emissivity of the shield. The plot is available only when "shield" is checked, and a 3D physical representation of the ball and shield is shown on the plot.

Details

The radiative heat transfer is calculated using a relation between the temperatures of the ball and enclosure over the sum of the resistances:

$$q_{\mathrm{shield}} = \frac{\sigma\,(T_{\mathrm{w}}^4 - T_{\mathrm{b}}^4)} {R_{\mathrm{w+s}} + R_{\mathrm{s,i}} + R_{\mathrm{s,o}} + R_{\mathrm{b+s}} + R_{\mathrm{b}}},$$

$$q_{\mathrm{no\ shield}} = \frac{\sigma\,(T_{\mathrm{w}}^4 - T_{\mathrm{b}}^4)} {R_{\mathrm{b+s}} + R_{\mathrm{b}}},$$

where $q$ is in $\mathrm{W/m^2}$, $\sigma$ is the Stefan–Boltzmann constant ($\mathrm{W/[m^2\,K^4]}$), $T_{\mathrm{w}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{b}}$ are the wall and ball temperatures (K) and $R$ is thermal resistance ($\mathrm{m^{-2}}$).

The resistance from enclosure to shield is:

$$R_{\mathrm{w+s}} = 1/A_{\mathrm{s}},$$

where $A_{\mathrm{s}} = \pi d_{\mathrm{s}}^2 = \pi (d_{\mathrm{b}} + 0.2)^2$.

The resistances associated with shield emissivity are:

$$R_{\mathrm{s,i}} = \frac{1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{s,i}}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{s,i}}\,A_{\mathrm{s}}},$$

$$R_{\mathrm{s,o}} = \frac{1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{s,o}}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{s,o}}\,A_{\mathrm{s}}}.$$

The resistance between the shield and ball is:

$$R_{\mathrm{b+s}} = 1/A_{\mathrm{b}},$$

where $A_{\mathrm{b}} = \pi d_{\mathrm{b}}^2$.

The resistance associated with the ball emissivity is:

$$R_{\mathrm{b}} = \frac{1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{b}}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{b}}\,A_{\mathrm{b}}},$$

where $A$ is surface area ($\mathrm{m^2}$), $d$ is diameter (m) and $\epsilon$ is emissivity.

Reference

[1] T. L. Bergman, A. S. Lavine, F. P. Incropera and D. P. DeWitt, Introduction to Heat Transfer, 6th ed., Hoboken, NJ: John Wiley and Sons, 2011.

Symbols

$A_{\mathrm{b}}$ = surface area of ball ($\mathrm{m^2}$)

$A_{\mathrm{s}}$ = surface area of shield ($\mathrm{m^2}$)

$d_{\mathrm{b}}$ = diameter of ball (m)

$d_{\mathrm{s}}$ = diameter of shield (m)

$q$ = net radiative heat flow from wall to ball ($\mathrm{W/m^2}$)

$R_{\mathrm{w+s}}$ = space resistance between wall and shield ($\mathrm{m^{-2}}$)

$R_{\mathrm{s,o}}$ = surface resistance of outer shield surface ($\mathrm{m^{-2}}$)

$R_{\mathrm{s,i}}$ = surface resistance of inner shield surface ($\mathrm{m^{-2}}$)

$R_{\mathrm{b+s}}$ = space resistance between shield and ball ($\mathrm{m^{-2}}$)

$R_{\mathrm{b}}$ = surface resistance of ball ($\mathrm{m^{-2}}$)

$T_{\mathrm{w}}$ = wall (enclosure) temperature, 600 K

$T_{\mathrm{b}}$ = ball temperature (K)

$\epsilon_{\mathrm{b}}$ = emissivity of ball

$\epsilon_{\mathrm{s,i}}$ = emissivity of inner shield surface

$\epsilon_{\mathrm{s,o}}$ = emissivity of outer shield surface

$\sigma$ = Stefan–Boltzmann constant, $5.67\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm{W/(m^2\,K^4)}$
