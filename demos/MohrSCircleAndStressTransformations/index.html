Directions

This Demonstration uses a Mohr's circle and a stress plot to show the effects of stress transformation on a differential element or area. Use sliders to vary the stresses \(\sigma_x\), \(\sigma_y\) and \(\tau_{xy}\), which correspond to the stresses on the differential element at \(\theta = 0^{\circ}\) (dashed black line). Select "set angle" to vary the angle \(\theta\) with a slider. Stress transformation equations are used to compute the transformed stresses \(\sigma_{x'}\), \(\sigma_{y'}\) and \(\tau_{x'y'}\) (solid black line), which are shown on the differential stress element as blue, green and black arrows, respectively. Select the "Mohr's circle" button to display Mohr's circle on the left. An angle of \(\theta\) on the differential stress element corresponds to an angle of \(2\theta\) on the Mohr's circle. Select the "stress plot" button to display a plot of all three transformed stresses versus \(\theta\) on the left. Select "set angle" to set \(\theta\) with a slider or "critical stresses" to snap the diagram to the angle of one of the principal stresses (\(\sigma_1\) and \(\sigma_2\)) or one of the maximum in-plane shear stresses (\(+\tau_{max}\) and \(-\tau_{max}\)). Note that Mohr's circle is displayed with positive \(+\tau_{xy}\) in the downward direction, so an increase in \(\theta\) results in a counterclockwise rotation on both the Mohr's circle and the differential stress element.

Details

The transformed stresses are calculated from the normal stresses at \(\theta = 0^{\circ}\):

\(\sigma_{x'} = \sigma_{ave} + \dfrac{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}{2}\cos(2\,\theta) + \tau_{xy}\sin(2\,\theta)\),
\(\sigma_{y'} = \sigma_{ave} - \dfrac{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}{2}\cos(2\,\theta) - \tau_{xy}\sin(2\,\theta)\),
\(\tau_{x'y'} = -\dfrac{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}{2}\sin(2\,\theta) + \tau_{xy}\cos(2\,\theta)\),

where \(\sigma_{ave} = \dfrac{\sigma_x + \sigma_y}{2}\) is the average of the \(x\) and \(y\) stresses, \(\sigma_x\) and \(\sigma_y\) are the normal stresses in the \(x\) and \(y\) directions and \(\tau_{xy}\) is the shear stress (MPa) at \(\theta = 0^{\circ}\). Here \(\theta\) is the angle of rotation (positive in the counterclockwise direction), \(\sigma_{x'}\) and \(\sigma_{y'}\) are the normal stresses in the \(x\) and \(y\) directions (MPa) and \(\tau_{x'y'}\) is the shear stress (MPa).

The principal stresses occur for \(\tau_{x'y'} = 0\):

\(\sigma_1 = \sigma_{ave} + R\),
\(\sigma_2 = \sigma_{ave} - R\),
\(\theta_{\mathrm{p}1} = \dfrac{1}{2}\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{2\,\tau_{xy}}{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}\right)\),
\(\theta_{\mathrm{p}2} = \theta_{\mathrm{p}1} + 90^{\circ}\),
\(R = \sqrt{\left(\dfrac{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}{2}\right)^{2} + \tau_{xy}^{2}}\),

where \(\sigma_1\) and \(\sigma_2\) are the first and second principal stresses (MPa), \(\theta_{\mathrm{p}1}\) and \(\theta_{\mathrm{p}2}\) are the first and second principal angles and \(R\) is the radius of Mohr's circle (MPa). By convention, the right-hand principal stress on the Mohr's circle is denoted as \(\sigma_1\).

The maximum in-plane shear stress is \(\tau_{max} = R\) and the maximum shear angle is \(\theta_{\mathrm{s}} = \dfrac{1}{2}\tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{\sigma_y - \sigma_x}{2\,\tau_{xy}}\right)\).
