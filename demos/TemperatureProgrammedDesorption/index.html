Directions

During temperature-programmed desorption (TPD), a molecule is adsorbed on a solid surface, the temperature is ramped linearly with time, and the rate of desorption is measured. The rates of desorption are plotted versus either temperature or time (select with buttons) either for three heating rates (1, 3 and 10 K/s) at one initial coverage, or for three initial fractional surface coverages (θ0 = 0.1, 0.5 and 1.0) at one heating rate; choose which with the compare buttons. The slider for the quantity being compared is inactive. Select the desorption order with buttons; vary the desorption activation energy, the pre-exponential factor, the initial fractional surface coverage (θ0) and the heating rate with sliders.

Details

As the heating rate increases, the temperature at which the desorption rate is a maximum (peak temperature) increases, and the maximum rate of desorption increases. When plotted on a time scale, as the heating rate increases, the maximum rate of desorption occurs at shorter times. As the activation energy for desorption increases, the peak temperature increases. This is one of the most widely-used techniques for characterizing catalysts and porous materials and for studying catalytic mechanisms.

Mole balance: dθ/dt = −A e−Ea/(RT) θn,

where θ is the fractional surface coverage, t is time (s), at t = 0, θ = θ0 is the initial fractional surface coverage, n is the desorption order, A is the pre-exponential factor (s−1), Ea is the activation energy (kJ/mol), R is the ideal gas constant (kJ/(mol K)) and T is temperature (K). The desorption rate, −dθ/dt, has units of s−1.

Temperature-time relation: dT/dt = β,

where β is the heating rate (K/s).
