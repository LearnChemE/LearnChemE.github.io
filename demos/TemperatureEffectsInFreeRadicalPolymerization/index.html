Directions

This simulation shows why increasing the temperature of a free-radical polymerization increases the rate of polymerization, \(R_p\), but decreases the number-average molecular weight, \(M_n\). The rate constants for initiator decomposition (\(k_d\)), propagation (\(k_p\)), and termination (\(k_t\)) each follow the Arrhenius equation with their own activation energy. Because these rate constants combine differently in \(R_p\) and in \(M_n\), the overall activation energy is positive for \(R_p\) and negative for \(M_n\).

Use the sliders to change the temperature, the initiator concentration, and the activation energies \(E_d\), \(E_p\), and \(E_t\). Select whether chains terminate by combination or by disproportionation.

The plot on the left shows \(R_p\) (left axis) and \(M_n\) (right axis) versus temperature on logarithmic axes; the dashed vertical line marks the selected temperature. Click "Arrhenius plot" to see the natural log of each quantity divided by its value at 60 °C versus \(1000/T\); the slope of each line is \(-E/R\).

The bar chart on the right shows how the three activation energies add to give the overall activation energies for \(R_p\) and for \(X_n\). One-half of \(E_d\) is added for \(R_p\) but subtracted for \(X_n\). Decrease \(E_d\) toward zero (as for photoinitiation) to see when the molecular weight increases with temperature.

Details

Each rate constant follows the Arrhenius equation, written relative to a reference temperature \(T_\mathrm{ref}\) = 60 °C:

\[ k_i = k_{i,\mathrm{ref}}\exp\!\left[-\frac{E_i}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T}-\frac{1}{T_\mathrm{ref}}\right)\right], \qquad i = d,\,p,\,t \]

Initiation, propagation, and termination steps:

\[ \mathrm{I}\xrightarrow{k_d}2\,\mathrm{R}\cdot \] \[ \mathrm{R}\cdot+\mathrm{M}\xrightarrow{k_p}\mathrm{R}\cdot \] \[ \mathrm{R}\cdot+\mathrm{R}\cdot\xrightarrow{k_t}\text{dead polymer} \]

The steady-state approximation sets the rate of radical initiation equal to the rate of termination:

\[ R_i = 2fk_d[\mathrm{I}] = 2k_t[\mathrm{R}\cdot]^2 \] \[ [\mathrm{R}\cdot] = \left(\frac{fk_d[\mathrm{I}]}{k_t}\right)^{1/2} \]

The rate of polymerization is:

\[ R_p = k_p[\mathrm{M}][\mathrm{R}\cdot] = k_p\left(\frac{fk_d}{k_t}\right)^{1/2}[\mathrm{M}][\mathrm{I}]^{1/2} \]

The kinetic chain length is the rate of propagation divided by the rate of initiation:

\[ \nu = \frac{R_p}{R_i} = \frac{k_p[\mathrm{M}]}{2\left(fk_dk_t[\mathrm{I}]\right)^{1/2}} \]

The number-average degree of polymerization and molecular weight are:

\[ X_n = 2\nu \quad \text{(combination)}, \qquad X_n = \nu \quad \text{(disproportionation)} \] \[ M_n = X_n M_0 \]

Collecting the rate constants shows how each activation energy enters:

\[ R_p \propto k_p\,k_d^{1/2}\,k_t^{-1/2} \] \[ E_{R_p} = E_p + \tfrac{1}{2}E_d - \tfrac{1}{2}E_t \] \[ X_n \propto k_p\,k_d^{-1/2}\,k_t^{-1/2} \] \[ E_{X_n} = E_p - \tfrac{1}{2}E_d - \tfrac{1}{2}E_t \]

For thermal initiators such as AIBN or benzoyl peroxide, \(E_d\) ≈ 120–150 kJ/mol, much larger than \(E_p\) ≈ 20–40 kJ/mol and \(E_t\) ≈ 5–20 kJ/mol. Thus \(E_{R_p}\) is large and positive (≈ 80–90 kJ/mol), so \(R_p\) increases rapidly with temperature, whereas \(E_{X_n}\) is negative, so \(X_n\) and \(M_n\) decrease. A higher temperature generates radicals faster, so \([\mathrm{R}\cdot]\) increases. More radicals consume monomer faster, but they also terminate each other more often, so each chain adds fewer monomer units before it terminates.

\(E_{X_n}\) is positive only if \(E_d < 2E_p - E_t\), as for photoinitiation or radiation initiation, where \(E_d\) ≈ 0.

The parameters at \(T_\mathrm{ref}\) = 60 °C are representative of bulk styrene with AIBN initiator:

\(k_{d,\mathrm{ref}}\)9.5 × 10−6 s−1
\(k_{p,\mathrm{ref}}\)341 L mol−1 s−1
\(k_{t,\mathrm{ref}}\)6.0 × 107 L mol−1 s−1
\(f\)0.6
\([\mathrm{M}]\)8.35 mol/L
\(M_0\)104.15 g/mol

The model assumes low conversion (constant \([\mathrm{M}]\) and \([\mathrm{I}]\)), a temperature-independent \(f\), no chain transfer, and no gel effect.
