Directions

This simulator plots real-time air states on a psychrometric chart and lets you model common HVAC air-conditioning processes.

1. Set your conditions

  • Enter the site Elevation (top of page) to set the local atmospheric pressure used in every calculation. Switch units with the Metric (SI) / Imperial (I-P) toggle.

2. Define State 1 and State 2

  • Use the State 1 / State 2 tabs (right panel) to choose which air state you are editing.
  • Pick a property pair from Calculate via (dry bulb + RH, wet bulb, dew point, or humidity ratio) and type values into the two input boxes, or drag the State 1 or State 2 marker on the chart (whichever state is selected in the panel on the right) to a new location. Every other property (RH, W, Twb, Tdp, h, v) is solved automatically and shown in the Calculated state properties cards.
  • Choose a Process mode (sensible heating/cooling, cooling & dehumidification, evaporative cooling, or adiabatic mixing) to draw the corresponding process path between State 1 and State 2 on the chart.
    Sensible heating or cooling occurs when energy is added or removed without moisture transfer, so the path is at constant humidity ratio (horizontal).
    Cooling and dehumidification occurs as warm, moist air cools below its dew point so water condenses and the overall humidity ratio is reduced. State 2 is not at full saturation because only part of the air stream contacts the coil fins.
    Adiabatic humidification occurs when liquid water is injected into dry air. The water evaporates, using sensible heat from the air. This lowers the dry bulb temperature and raises the humidity along a constant enthalpy line.
    Adiabatic mixing models the mixing of two air streams (e.g., outside air and return air in an HVAC system). The mixed state is located directly on the line connecting state 1 and state 2.
  • When imperial units are selected, the Humidity ratio is in grains of water vapor per lb of dry air, where 7,000 grains = 1.0 lb.

3. Explore the chart

  • Drag inside the plot area to pan; use the scroll wheel (or pinch on touch devices) to zoom.
  • Move your mouse over the chart to see live properties update in the panel above the chart, with a crosshair marker at your cursor location.
  • Click a button under "Click to highlight chart lines" to emphasize the saturation curve, constant-RH lines, constant wet-bulb lines, constant-enthalpy lines, or constant-volume lines. Click the active button again (or click another one) to turn the highlight off.

Use the Details button for the governing equations and symbol definitions, and Guided inquiry to download a companion worksheet.

Details: equations & symbols

All properties are computed at the local total pressure \(P\), determined from elevation \(z\) (m) using the barometric formula:

\[ P(z) = 101.325\left(1 - 2.25577\times10^{-5}\,z\right)^{5.25588} \ \text{kPa} \]

Saturation vapor pressure

A Tetens-type correlation is used for dry-bulb temperature \(T\) in \(^{\circ}\text{C}\):

\[ p_{sat}(T) = 0.61078\,\exp\!\left(\dfrac{17.27\,T}{T+237.3}\right)\ \text{kPa}, \quad T \ge 0^{\circ}\text{C} \]

\[ p_{sat}(T) = 0.61078\,\exp\!\left(\dfrac{21.875\,T}{T+265.5}\right)\ \text{kPa}, \quad T < 0^{\circ}\text{C (over ice)} \]

Humidity ratio and vapor pressure

\[ W = 0.62198\,\dfrac{p_w}{P-p_w} \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad p_w = \dfrac{P\,W}{0.62198+W} \]

Relative humidity

\[ \text{RH} = \dfrac{p_w}{p_{sat}(T_{db})}\times 100\% \]

Dew point temperature

Found by inverting the Tetens correlation for the vapor pressure \(p_w\):

\[ T_{dp} = \dfrac{237.3\,\ln\!\left(p_w/0.61078\right)}{17.27-\ln\!\left(p_w/0.61078\right)} \]

Enthalpy of moist air

\[ h = 1.006\,T_{db} + W\left(2501.0+1.86\,T_{db}\right)\ \text{kJ/kg dry air} \]

Specific volume

\[ v = \dfrac{R_{da}\,(T_{db}+273.15)\,(1+1.6078\,W)}{P}, \qquad R_{da}=0.287055\ \text{kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)} \]

Thermodynamic wet-bulb temperature

\(T_{wb}\) is solved iteratively (bisection) from the adiabatic-saturation energy balance:

\[ h(T_{db},W) = h_{sat}(T_{wb}) - \left(W_{s}(T_{wb})-W\right)c_{p,w}\,T_{wb} \]

where \(W_s(T_{wb})\) is the saturation humidity ratio at \(T_{wb}\) and \(c_{p,w}=4.186\ \text{kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}\) is the specific heat of liquid water.

Adiabatic mixing of two streams

Conservation of dry-air mass and energy for streams 1 and 2 combining into stream 3 (with mass fractions \(r_1+r_2=1\)):

\[ T_{db,3}=r_1T_{db,1}+r_2T_{db,2}, \qquad W_3=r_1W_1+r_2W_2 \]
