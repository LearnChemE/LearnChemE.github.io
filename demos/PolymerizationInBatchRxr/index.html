Select "step growth" or "free radical" polymerization with the buttons, and use the sliders to set reaction rate constants, initial concentrations, and reaction time. The number average chain length N̄j, the weight average chain length W̄j, and the polydispersity are shown. The number fraction \(N_j\) and weight fraction \(W_j\) of polymer chains for a polymer with \(j\) monomer units are:

\[ N_j = \frac{y_j}{\sum y_j}, \qquad W_j = \frac{j\,y_j}{\sum j\,y_j}, \]

where \(y_j\) is the mole fraction and \(p\) is the fraction of functional groups that have reacted:

\[ y_j = (1-p)\,p^{\,j-1}. \]

For step-growth polymerization:

\[ p = 1 - \left(M_0 k_p t + 1\right)^{-1}, \qquad M = M_0(1-p). \]

For free-radical polymerization:

\[ p = \frac{k_p M}{k_p M + k_t \tau + \sqrt{2 k_t k_i f I_0 e^{-k_i t}}}, \]

\[ M = M_0 \exp\!\left[\, 2 k_p \sqrt{\frac{2 f I_0}{k_i k_t}}\,\left(e^{-k_i t/2} - 1\right) \right], \]

where \(M_0\) and \(I_0\) are the initial monomer and initiator concentrations; \(M\) is monomer concentration; \(k_p\), \(k_i\) and \(k_t\) are the rate constants for the reaction, initiation and termination; \(t\) is time; \(\tau\) is the terminator concentration and \(f\) is fraction of chains initiated by initiator.

The number \(\bar{N}_j\) and weight \(\bar{W}_j\) average chain lengths are:

\[ \bar{N}_j = \frac{\sum j\,P_j}{\sum P_j}, \qquad \bar{W}_j = \frac{\sum j^{2} P_j}{\sum j\,P_j}, \]

where \(P_j = y_j M\) is the polymer concentration.

The polydispersity \(D\) is:

\[ D = \frac{\bar{W}_j}{\bar{N}_j}. \]

In step-growth polymerization, chains grow by reaction between functional groups on any two molecules: monomers, oligomers, or polymers. A typical example is a condensation polymerization, such as a diol reacting with a diacid to form a polyester and water:

\[ \mathrm{HO{-}R{-}OH} + \mathrm{HOOC{-}R'{-}COOH} \rightarrow \mathrm{HO{-}R{-}OOC{-}R'{-}COOH} + \mathrm{H_2O} \]

The product still has a reactive group at each end, so it continues to react. Typical steps are:

  1. monomer + monomer → dimer: \(\;M_1 + M_1 \xrightarrow{k_p} P_2\)
  2. dimer + monomer → trimer: \(\;P_2 + M_1 \xrightarrow{k_p} P_3\)
  3. dimer + dimer → tetramer: \(\;P_2 + P_2 \xrightarrow{k_p} P_4\)
  4. in general, any two chains combine: \(\;P_i + P_j \xrightarrow{k_p} P_{i+j}\)

Each step also releases a small molecule (H2O in this example). Assuming the rate constant \(k_p\) is the same for every step (equal reactivity), the concentration of unreacted functional groups \(M\) decreases as

\[ -\frac{dM}{dt} = k_p M^2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad M = \frac{M_0}{1 + k_p M_0 t}, \]

which gives the conversion \(p = 1 - M/M_0\) used in Details. Monomer is consumed quickly, but long chains form only at very high conversion: the number average chain length is \(\bar{N}_j = 1/(1-p)\), so \(p = 0.99\) is needed for \(\bar{N}_j = 100\).

Free-radical polymerization is a chain-growth process: a radical adds monomer units one at a time, rapidly building a long chain until the radical is destroyed. Typical steps are:

  1. Initiation. The initiator (for example, a peroxide or AIBN) decomposes into two radicals, and a fraction \(f\) of these radicals starts chains by adding to a monomer:

    \[ I \xrightarrow{k_i} 2R^{\bullet}, \qquad R^{\bullet} + M \rightarrow P_1^{\bullet} \]

  2. Propagation. The growing radical chain adds monomer units, one at a time. This step repeats hundreds to thousands of times for each chain:

    \[ P_j^{\bullet} + M \xrightarrow{k_p} P_{j+1}^{\bullet} \]

  3. Termination. Two growing radicals react with each other (by combination or disproportionation), or a growing radical reacts with a terminator \(T\), producing "dead" polymer that no longer grows:

    \[ P_j^{\bullet} + P_k^{\bullet} \xrightarrow{k_t} \text{dead polymer}, \qquad P_j^{\bullet} + T \rightarrow P_j \]

The radical concentration is small and nearly constant (quasi-steady state), because radicals are formed and destroyed at about the same rate:

\[ [P^{\bullet}] = \sqrt{\frac{2 f k_i I}{k_t}}, \qquad I = I_0\, e^{-k_i t}. \]

Monomer is consumed by propagation, \(-dM/dt = k_p M [P^{\bullet}]\), which integrates to the equation for \(M\) in Details. The probability \(p\) that a growing chain adds another monomer instead of terminating is the propagation rate divided by the sum of the propagation and termination rates, which gives the equation for \(p\) in Details. Unlike step-growth polymerization, long chains form right away, even at low monomer conversion.
